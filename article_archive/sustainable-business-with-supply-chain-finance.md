Historical quotes, supposedly from wise and amazing people, have become like drugs for the disillusioned and broken. You hear these seductive little strings of word porn all the time by people who are trying to convince you of something, and so they invoke the faux wisdom of someone else who was supposedly a lot smarter than both of you.

There are, indeed, many real and excellent quotes out there, by truly brilliant minds. Unfortunately, when it comes to self-help, motivation, and productivity coaches, those quotes get entirely ignored, because they require critical thinking.

Instead, the false pushers of blind-faith-inspiration and spiritual-self-aggrandizing tend to spew bite-sized quotes by people who are popularly considered to have been wise, or otherwise extremely important and successful.

The problem is that these quotes are always either taken out of context, attributed to the wrong people, or are entirely fabricated. The result is that someone thinks they are receiving wisdom from someone credible and noble, when in fact, it could be the words of a murderous rapist who was obsessed with penis art.

Below are a few popular examples:

## Albert Einstein

> “The definition of insanity is doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results.” — Not Einstein

It’s important to note that this is not the definition of insanity — it’s also important to mention that Albert Einstein never said anything like this, ever.

This famous quote was actually coined in the [early 1980s by either Alcoholics Anonymous or Narcotics Anonymous](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2017/03/23/same/). Indeed, it has a nice ring when you say it to a drug addict, but it makes no sense in any other context.

According to the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/insanity), the actual definition is this:

> ***Insanity:***

> ***1)*** *The condition of being seriously mentally ill.*

> ***2)*** *An action that is stupid and likely to have extremely bad results.*

For those dealing with addiction, the second definition makes sense since their repeated actions of using and/or drinking are stupid, and will likely result in an extremely negative outcome.

However, in all other contexts, repeating the same thing over and over again is actually the path to improvement — we call this practice. In many eastern religions, this is also the path to enlightenment — they call this meditation.

Practice and mediation are good. Examples of things that require practice include learning how to play a musical instrument, training for sports, learning math, and learning a new language. Examples of the benefits of meditation include lowered stress levels, better sleep, and reduced anxiety.

In other words, this misattributed nugget of wisdom was meant for the most desperate of people who were struggling with extreme addition — it was perhaps attributed to Einstein [because his good friend and intellectual sparring partner, Niels Bohr, said something similar, albeit with an entirely different meaning:](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/einstein-s-parable-of-quantum-insanity/)

> “Naïveté is doing the same thing over and over, and always expecting the same result.” — Niels Bohr

Again, Bohr said basically the opposite of the popular self-help insanity quote, since Bohr knew that the universe was oftentimes unpredictable and chaotic. In regards to self-help, the quote misattributed to Einstein is entirely meaningless.

## Archilochus

According to [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.fr/), self-help guru Tim Ferriss “[lives his life according to an ancient Greek quote that helps him prepare for the worst](https://www.businessinsider.fr/us/tim-ferriss-favorite-quote-greek-philosopher-archilochus-2017-12).”

That quote is:

> “We don’t rise to the level of our expectations. We fall to the level of our training.” — Probably Not Archilochus

Aside from the fact that he was likely a rather wretched human being, the self-help pushers who love to cite this ancient womanizer (who died over 2500 years ago) should know that Archilochus never actually said anything remotely close to this.

Who was [Archilochus](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Archilochus-Greek-author), and why are [so many self-help gurus all about him](https://www.businessinsider.fr/us/tim-ferriss-favorite-quote-greek-philosopher-archilochus-2017-12)?

Archilochus was the son of a wealthy noble from Paros, an island known for expensive and fine marble. Archilochus spent much of his privileged life writing poetry, drinking wine, having sex, and [killing for sport and profit](https://www.ancientworldmagazine.com/articles/archilochus-poet-mercenary/). Perhaps the greatest contribution that he gave to the modern world was the word “*tyrant,*” which is derived from a defamatory nickname he gave to the leader of a rival kingdom.

Most of the writing from Archilochus was loaded with sexual references and ferociously abusive language about others. Perhaps the best example was a poem he wrote about how he crudely rejected his fiancé and instead seduced her sister. [The poem was so sexually explicit and shameful, that both women and their father committed suicide because of it](https://briefpoems.wordpress.com/tag/archilochus/).

Archilochus was a member of the elite who was concerned mostly with politics and war, but who also composed a plethora of erotic songs and other salacious materials, of which many of his male peers found quite enjoyable.

[Here are some examples](https://briefpoems.wordpress.com/tag/archilochus/):

> His prick … swelled like that of a Perinea grain-fed breeding ass.

> He comes, in bed  
> As copiously as  
> A Prienian ass  
> And is equipped  
> Like a stallion

> His penis is swollen  
> like a donkey from Priene  
> taking his fill of barley.

> She is a common woman for rent,  
> but what sensuality and fat ankles,  
> O fat whore for hire.

The quote that today’s self-help guru’s attribute to Archilochus is so different from everything else that he wrote, that it makes no sense that he mysteriously and suddenly excreted a bit of contemporary-sounding inspiration that sounds uncannily similar to all the other rubbish that comes out of the modern self-help industry.

Even if he did say it, Archilochus was definitely no role model, even if he was Greek.

## Mahatma Gandhi

> “Be the change you wish to see in the world” — Not Ghandi

The idea behind this misquote is obvious; take control of your own life and project outward what you want others to do. In other words, “*lead by example*.”

That’s all fine and well, but *lead by example* was actually coined by [Albert Schweitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Schweitzer#Colonialism), who was actually a pretty amazing guy, however, what he actually said was:

> “The three most important ways to lead people are: by example… by example… by example… ” — Albert Schweitzer

This is quite a bit different than the common directive of “lead by example,” which entirely misses the context of Schweitzer’s life, which was primarily dedicated to helping improve the lives of other people, and also developing important advancements in medicine, music, and math. Schweitzer was not a businessman, nor did he die laying upon mountains of cash or multiple properties in all the hottest neighborhoods.

Schweitzer did, however, receive the Nobel Peace Prize in 1952 for his work against nuclear tests and nuclear weapons — this is probably what he meant when he said that the most important way to lead people is by example.

Just like Albert Schweitzer, Mahatma Gandhi was also slightly misquoted, because what he actually said was:

> “If we could change ourselves, the tendencies in the world would also change. As a man changes his own nature, so does the attitude of the world change towards him. … We need not wait to see what others do.” — Actually Gandhi

The common misquote, “*be the change you wish to see in the world*,” is too simple to fully grasp what Gandhi actually meant. Gandhi, who was facing down the terrifying might of the racist and imperialist British Empire, was trying to inspire his followers to motivate them to join him in resisting the will of one of the most dangerous and powerful empires the world had ever known.

Gandhi was not telling modern Americans to change their attitudes about their jobs and then everything will be okay, and he was not telling corporate managers to lead their disillusioned staff, by example.

## Niccolò Machiavelli

> “The ends justify the means.” — Not Machiavelli

If you’re looking for historical guidance, most ethical people agree that Machiavelli is probably not the right person. However, even if you are an overly testosteronal capitalist alpha male who actually believes that the purpose of being a man is to get rich and date a fashion model, you should probably know that this phrase was actually coined by [Sergey Nechayev](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Nechayev), a murderous Russian anarchist revolutionary terrorist and leader of Nihilism who lived during the 19th century.

Machiavelli did say, “*one must consider the final result*,” which is quite a bit different, and actually makes quite a lot of sense.

Nechayev said “*the ends justify the means*” as a motto for any time he needed to excuse his horrific actions, which included blackmail, torture, kidnapping, and murder. Nechayev also said this:

> “A revolutionary is a doomed man. He has no private interests, no affairs, sentiments, ties, property nor even a name of his own. His entire being is devoured by one purpose, one thought, one passion — the revolution. Heart and soul, not merely by word but by deed, he has severed every link with the social order and with the entire civilized world; with the laws, good manners, conventions, and morality of that world. He is its merciless enemy and continues to inhabit it with only one purpose — to destroy it.” — [Sergey Nechayev](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Nechayev)

Nechayev planned and carried out the brutal murder of a young student named Ivanov, who had merely questioned his ideas. In 1869, Nechayev and several of his comrades beat, strangled, and shot Ivanov, then hid his body in a frozen lake by forcing it through a hole in the ice. Despite their attempts to hide their crime, Ivanov’s body was found, and so Nechayev quickly fled the country before he could be arrested. Three years later, Nechayev was found in [Zurich](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich) and subsequently arrested and handed over to the Russian police — he died in a cold prison cell in 1882.

It’s also interesting to note that Vladimir Lenin said that he was very influenced by Nechayev and that Lenin quite consciously put into practice what Nechayev had taught.

Next time you’re considering uttering this popular phrase as an excuse for bad behavior, remember that if you are a capitalist, the person who actually created this quote would have enjoyed killing you slowly.

## The Bible

> “Money is the root of all evil.” — Not The Bible

What the King James Version of The Bible actually said was this:

> “ For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.” — 1 Timothy 6:10, KJV

There is an important distinction here that is commonly confused: It is possible for people to have honest and beneficial exchanges without using “dirty evil money.” That said, people who obsess about money, and love it so much that they do anything to get it, tend to engage in evil acts.

The Bible calls this “Greed.”

If you are justifying your greedy actions by saying that it is just the nature of money, just know that no one ever said that.

## Charles R. Swindoll

> “Life is 10% what happens to you and 90% how you react.” — Charles R. Swindoll

Speaking of The Bible, Mr. Swindoll isn’t necessarily a bad person, but he definitely isn’t a good one. While this quote is actually exactly what Charles Swindoll said, people who quote him rarely have any idea who he is.

Charles “Chuck” Swindoll is an [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) Christian pastor, author, educator, and radio preacher. Chuck grew up in Texas before joining the U.S. Marines during peacetime, where his primary role was in the marching band. After the military, Chuck moved back to Texas to attend Seminary School — fast forward and Chuck has published over 70 books and preaches his version of Jesus to several hundred thousand, which is also probably how much he earns each fiscal quarter.

In other words, Charles Swindoll is not someone who can relate to the reality of 90% of people.

## Putting it all together

There are many powerful quotes that are real, and that are relevant to the challenges and experiences of today. Some of these quotes are very old, while others are relatively recent — just because someone is alive today doesn’t mean that they can’t have as much wisdom as someone who is dead.

The unfortunate reality is that people who are trying to sell an idea, or a product, have deliberately twisted historical quotes and taken them out of context in order to serve their own agenda of selling more books.

Next time you hear a quote from a self-help or lifestyle guru, instead [consider this actual quote from George Orwell](https://www.newoxfordreview.org/documents/giving-an-appearance-of-solidity-to-pure-wind/):

> “Political language is designed to make lies sound truthful and murder respectable, and to give an appearance of solidity to pure wind.”
