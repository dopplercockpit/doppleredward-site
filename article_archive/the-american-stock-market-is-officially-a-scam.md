## Tesla & Tesla

Tesla is named after a person, Nikola Tesla, an actual inventor who actually invented something. Tesla, Inc. is a company from California that basically stole [someone else’s idea](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/873116351316938753) and figured out how to convince a crazy rich person (Elon Musk) to get involved and nearly single-handedly finance it. Afterwards, the company ran at a loss for 18 years until finally earning money in 2020, [thanks to government subsidies in the USA and China.](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-tesla-china-tax-idUSKCN1VK1AU)

For 18 years the company was not profitable, yet the wealth of Elon Musk continued to soar to new heights, making him one of the richest people in the world.

The real Tesla, the man, was a bit unhinged himself and perhaps wasn’t that different than Elon Musk. Tesla managed to convince rail and finance tycoon J.P Morgan that he could build a trans-Atlantic wireless communication system that would outperform the existing short range radio system. Morgan gave Tesla $150,000 (roughly $4.5 million in 2020) in return for a 51% control of the patents for the new system — but almost as soon as the contract was signed, Tesla decided to include his ideas of terrestrial [wireless power transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_energy_transfer) instead, as *he* thought it was a more competitive system.

When Tesla erratically and suddenly changed the plan without discussing it with anyone else, Morgan considered Tesla to be in breach of contract and refused to provide any further funding — that was the smart thing to do. The project was subsequently abandoned and never became operational.

J.P. Morgan, a famously powerful man who made decisions rationally, had a code that he followed, and that included following through on promises, especially legal ones. But Morgan was powerful, and Morgan knew better.

How powerful? Well he was so powerful that he basically ran the American economy for several decades. The first time that J.P. Morgan bailed out the U.S. economy, was in 1893. America was quickly running out of money, and so President Grover Cleveland went and asked him to loan the U.S. treasury $65 million in gold in order to preserve the gold standard and prevent economic collapse. Morgan did it.

The second time was in 1907, when Morgan personally reorganized the U.S. economy and used his power to compel many national banks to bail out the bankrupt New York banks, and further loan the New York Stock Exchange $25 million. Without this bailout, the exchange would have shut down and the entire banking system would have been in a freefall.

Morgan was able to make this happen because he held an incredible amount of power over everyone else who was rich. During the 1907 crisis, Morgan ordered the biggest financial titans in America to his house on Madison Avenue in New York City, and he locked them in his personal study and told them that they couldn’t come out until they had agreed to a solution.

This of course was possible because Morgan could essentially make or break anyone, as he controlled the rails, he controlled financing, he controlled America.

If Morgan was around today, he would reject Elon Musk entirely. If Elon Musk ever becomes J.P. Morgan, well then we’re all doomed.
