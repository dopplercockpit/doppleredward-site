### Food & Beverage | Mixology

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*BiDsvYDvIeAwmiP-_9qKtQ.png)

Photo by [John Howland](http://johnhowlandphotography.com/)

At the height of my bartending career, I was in charge of an elite beverage program at an ultra-luxurious [Napa Valley](https://www.napavalley.com/) resort, that was once found hidden within a beautiful redwood forest, and frequently visited by some of the world’s most wealthy and famous people. I have many crazy stories from my time working there, such as going on an outrageous shopping spree with a large moving truck and a billionaire's credit card, hosting a private concert with some of the world’s most legendary songwriters, and the time when I witnessed the sale of the world’s [most expensive wine](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/auction-napa-valley-smashes-record-18511/) at a staggering $800,000!

In 2020, [much of the resort burned to the ground](https://napavalleyregister.com/news/local/napa-county-looks-at-meadowood-resort-rebuild/article_d99de626-fd17-5a3a-897b-0c8bc53d23e6.html) during a California Wildfire that consumed tens of thousands of acres, and so my memories are now just relics of a once great establishment.

While many of the tales I earned there are definitely for the ages, by far my favourite story was when we hosted a private event for the top 40 executives of the notorious investment firm, [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/).

One of the attendees would actually later become Donald Trump’s chief economic advisor.

During that night, I was tasked with creating one incredible and highly unique mixed drink that would be tray passed to the thirsty and controversial members of the global financial elite.

### Behind the cocktail

It was just a few years after the infamous financial collapse of 2008, and the chiefs of Goldman Sachs had become infamous for using [government bailout money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubled_Asset_Relief_Program) to earn themselves hundreds of millions in profit. In short, because of the infusion of taxpayer money, Goldman Sachs was able to undercut its competitors and establish its dominance as the second largest investment bank in the world, which is a status they continue to enjoy today. At the time, many people were suffering terribly and losing their homes, meanwhile, Goldman Sachs experienced massive growth and increased profits.

In other words, the 40 people in the room that night all had recently made large fortunes as a result of the great recession.

Today, Goldman Sachs manages over $900 billion of assets around the world, and its officers are considered to be globally powerful and influential. For the record, Goldman Sachs did, in fact, pay back the government in full with a generous 23% interest rate. Regardless, the ethics of their actions remain a debated topic.

The event that I worked that evening was for the top brass of Goldman, which at the time included both Goldman CEO Lloyd Blankfein and COO Gary Cohn, both of whom actually hovered around my bar the entire night.

The event itself was held in an exclusive building known as “The Wine Reserve,” a place where only the extremely rich could store their most private selections of wine, which had been custom-made by some of the industry’s top winemakers. The focus of the event that evening was the release of one of those red wines, which was paired with a delectable dinner created by one of the world’s best chefs.

My cocktail was meant to be both the opening and closing act.

### The priceless cocktail

Known simply as “Damiana,” [Liqueur de Damiana](https://www.damiana.net/) is an ancient Incan recipe that uses an herb of the same name, which itself has origins in the spiritual mythologies of the Incans. This is why the glass bottle for Damiana is modelled after a naked Incan Goddess.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*YUTB7WEjR2RBzzlCTOBjow.png)

Courtesy [Damiana Liqueur](https://www.damiana.net/)

Damiana was actually used to create the very first Margarita cocktail at [Hussong’s Cantina](http://cantinahussongs.com/home.html) in 1941. Legend states that a man named Don Carlos created the drink for the daughter of a German Ambassador who liked to eat the salt straight out of the dish at the bar. Perhaps Don Carlos was afraid to scold the daughter of an ambassador, or perhaps he fancied her, but either way, he devised a plan to prevent her from continuing to eat all of the salt that was meant for tequila drinkers, and thus he created a special drink for her with salt along the rim of the glass.

[Damiana](https://www.damiana.net/about-damiana/) was once used to treat sexual problems, and today it is still widely considered to be an aphrodisiac. We cannot know for sure if Don Carlos used Damiana out of necessity, or if it was because he wanted to serve the ambassador’s daughter an aphrodisiac, but regardless, the Margarita was first created using this unique liqueur.

As a nod to Don Carlos, that night, I decided to create a super unique variation of the super popular Mexican cocktail, one that was a nod to the roots of the original, but that also elevated the experience to a billionaire level.

I named the drink “Papalo” because the key ingredient was a rather obscure herb called pápalo, which is grown mostly in Mexico and Bolivia.

It was actually my highly discerning nightmare of a boss who decided to make it the featured drink for the Goldman execs, as it was his new favourite. The recipe was complicated and time-consuming since it called for the careful handling of delicate leaves, along with a laborious multi-step process that included foaming both pineapple and fresh egg whites.

Despite that, my boss wanted the drink to be passed around on trays, rather than be made to order. In short, this required mass producing these drinks, which was no simple feat.

Papalo looks similar to this, but with a leaf garnish — photo by [Vitchakorn Koonyosying](https://unsplash.com/photos/49zDZsYaug4?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/pineapple-cocktail?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

The first person to ever drink Papalo was my boss, and his response was so positive that I thought I was going to be promoted. I wasn’t, however, I was granted access to the private garden that housed the rare herbs and vegetables for the three-Michelin starred restaurant. This move by my boss created a bit of a power struggle between him and the chef, but I was able to procure the needed ingredients to successfully make the drink that evening.

Similar to the Classic Margarita, the key ingredients of Papalo were Tequila, Damiana and fresh squeezed key lime juice, however, the addition of caramelized pineapple, unique herbs, and fresh egg whites, is what made this drink so special.

The caramelized pineapplecame in the form of a purée, which was first frozen so that it would be cold and frothy when used, however, thawed just enough to be measured out perfectly.

The Tequila used was a Reposado, meaning that it was aged in oak barrels for around six months. This process mellows the otherwise vegetal liquor and imparts some interesting flavours and aromas, however, it doesn’t reduce the vegetal bite of the Tequila too much.

Additionally, aside from pápalo herb, I used [Lime Basil](https://homeguides.sfgate.com/grow-lime-basil-27325.html), which is a difficult-to-find and impossible to substitute ingredient that adds a slight tanginess.

The egg white was reverse dry shaken, which means that first the cocktail was shaken on ice without the egg, then the liquid was separated from the ice and shaken like crazy with the egg white. This is one reason why having cold purée was advantageous.

After everything was mixed, the drink was [fine strained](https://www.thespruceeats.com/how-to-strain-cocktails-759342), meaning that it was poured through a [passoire](https://www.thespruceeats.com/how-to-strain-cocktails-759342). This created a layered effect that allowed for the foam from the pineapple and egg to layer on top of the liquid and create a soft bed for a single leaf of pápalo.

The drink was very well received, and I was invited to make it again another time. I declined, however, because I didn’t receive a single dollar in tips the entire night, and instead learned that working for billionaires simple doesn’t pay.

I soon after quit bartending for good and instead enrolled in graduate school, but the memory of this cocktail lives on!

Due to the difficulty of getting all of these ingredients, this drink may never be created again, however, for those who want a taste of the high life, it’s certainly worth the attempt.
