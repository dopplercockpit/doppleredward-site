### The American economic model is not capitalism

America is not a pure capitalist society — it is a [*capitalist mixed economy*](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/mixed-economic-system.asp)with strong elements of [*corporatism*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporatism)*.*

The U.S. economic system subscribes to free-market capitalism in that it primarily leaves the means of production in the hands of private ownership, however, the American government enacts many subsidies that change natural supply and demand, and it also runs a number of publicly owned corporations.

Pure capitalist systems do not control the markets as much as the [American government does](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/economics/11/government-regulations.asp).

Who does have a pure capitalist system? Well, despite popular perception, [there is no known historical or modern economy that is pure capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) — all of the existing economic systems in the world currently under democratic governments fall somewhere along the *mixed continuum*.

### Mixed up perceptions

[Capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/020915/what-are-differences-between-capitalism-and-socialism.asp) is an economic system that functions within a market economy, where the state allows for the private ownership of capital goods.

That’s it.

Capitalism depends upon government regulation to assign and protect private ownership — without this regulation, nobody can really own anything.

Millions of Bernie Sanders supporters now openly decry Capitalism as inherently evil and call for the adoption of what he, and now they, label as Democratic Socialism*.*

Democratic Socialism has never in history ever existed in reality — in fact, there has never been a widely adopted economic system that has allowed for capital goods to be either left entirely to private ownership or entirely controlled by a government.

Democratic Capitalism,on the other hand***,*** is an economic system that combinesa liberal democratic political system with a capitalist economic system, therefore, it enables a capitalist economy that is subject to control by a political system that is supported by the majority.

*This* is what Bernie Sanders actually advocates for, *not Socialism.*

### Capitalism does not mean Corporatism

A [capitalist system](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) and a [free-market](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/freemarket.asp) system are both economic environments that are based on the law of [supply and demand](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/economics/11/intro-supply-demand.asp), but [they are not the same](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/042215/what-difference-between-capitalist-system-and-free-market-system.asp) thing. Both are market-driven economies where market factors determine supply and demand.

Both systems utilize a market-based approach to determine the price and production of goods and services, however, the key difference is that [capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) is focused on the *creation* of wealth and ownership, whereas a more pure free-market system is focused on the *exchange* of wealth, goods and services. *A pure free market* system allows for entirely free competition in the economy, without any intervention from outside forces, while capitalismrequiresprivate ownership and government regulation.

Modern [American Libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) are true supporters of a free-market system absent of capitalism, and thus advocate for the removal of most or all forms of government intervention in the markets.

The existing American-Corporatist focused economic model is essentially a combination of [mercantilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism) and [finance capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_capitalism) that treats a worker like a disposable cost commodity — *this* is what has created the culture of short-term earnings, not capitalism.

Short-term earnings are a legal and cultural issue that has to do with how lawmakers define value, and it should not be mistaken as a byproduct of any free-market system — capitalism has to do with how ownership is assigned and protected, not with how value is determined.

An example of short-term earning culture wreaking havoc on society is in the rapid expansion of global firms through mergers and acquisitions (M&A). Investors vote for M&A actions because it provides short-term earnings via the removal of [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost), however, the combining of firms does little to create actual new economic value — the only role capitalism plays here is that private firms and individuals have the ability to own capital goods.

It is corporatism that has created the environment where investors and stakeholders of large for profit firms determine value — despite what many Liberal Democrats might think, the Democratic party has done far more to promote this economic model in the USA than the the GOP.
