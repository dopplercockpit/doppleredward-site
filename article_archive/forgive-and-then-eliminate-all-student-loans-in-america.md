### Reality is real

The truth is that America is not fine, and the global economy is about to enter a major recession, with the situation in the U.S. looking to be particularly brutal. This isn’t “fearbait,” it’s simply the reality that is backed up by mountains of actual data and analysis of economic indicators, which have further been widely published in the top financial publications worldwide.

While there is nothing that can be done to avoid the impending economic downturn, people can, however, protect themselves from the reams of hype-driven nonsensical false promises of so-called cutting-edge technology that promises to make them wealthy digital nomads.

The smarter thing to do would be to acknowledge that the world is currently entering a series of major crises, and focus on planning for that, rather than trying to live like a cheesy character in a bad Netflix series.

The truth is that all this type of nonsense is just seductive bullshit spewed to get people's attention and clicks so that the creators can make more money for themselves. Productivity influencers are, in fact, exactly what they claim “fearbait” writers to be, except they just use false promises and optimism instead of negativity and alarmism.

But don’t take my word for it, let’s take a look at the record.

After reviewing some of the horrible advice of positivity prophets over just the past year, it is clear just how empty the promises and advice they offer can be.

Let’s look at some of the big failed promises from 2021.

### Ownership of emails just created more unread e-mails

The digital nomad gurus of 2021 wrote countless articles that gleefully fawned over the fact that so many social platforms were finally giving content creators access to the e-mails of their subscribers. Supposedly, this was going to be a game-changer.

The reason that this was supposed to make everyone more money is that writers and content creators could blast their viewers with mind-numbing positivity, directly into their inboxes.

These optimistic pseudo-media wizards even went so far as to say that Instagram removed the ability to see likes on a post because the leaders of that platform realized it was useless.

In reality, Meta ordered the removal of the like function to protect them from the liability of 13-year-old bulimia and suicide.

In the end, having access to e-mails did nothing except irritate readers and viewers, and instead resulted in a major uptick in the percentage of unread e-mails.

### Bitclout was exactly the disappointment we all knew it would be

Sorry to burst the bubble, but Blockchain technology is not the future. During the summer of 2021, a platform called Bitclout was proclaimed to be the future of monetizing content creation, and it was going to make a bunch of us rich.

Based on the very same wacko notions behind NFTs, the technology driving Bitclout was supposed to turn the profile of a content creator into a type of virtual stock that could be traded on a sort of digital exchange.

Of course, that exchange was owned by Bitclout.

When users invested in a stock, the price of that creator's name would go up, and when the price of the stock went up, the creator could benefit by selling some of it into the nonexistent marketplace.

> “The fact a writer can now have their own cryptocurrency and have readers buy it as an investment in their future is huge. Writers can choose to give readers that become investors membership rewards (think Patreon).

> Writers are now stocks readers can invest in. Mind blown.”

Seems neat right? Wait, there’s more.

Users of Bitclout could also “diamond like” content, which gave the creator something called ”clout” — clout then could be traded in for U.S. dollars.

Of course, no misguided elitist tech platform would be complete without some kind of highly evolved progressive form of economic exchange, and so of course, Bitclout had a gift economy component to it.

In order to promote their vapid creations, content creators could “gift” their stocks to others as a form of marketing, in the hopes that people would buy more. Proponents of this idea heralded it as “free money” that users who grow their following would benefit from since more followers would mean more eyeballs, thus the incentive for other creators to generously heave their brilliance upon them at no cost.

Guess what, Bitclout didn’t work, and today it’s failing along with most other blockchain technology-based scams. The reason why it’s failing is the same reason that all blockchain-based ideas are failing — they are walking hypocrisies that are trying to decentralize and deregulate at the same time they want to make everyone rich.

Despite reality, expect the pushers of moronic optimism to soon promote something similar on a platform near you, because according to them, a bad market is actually a good thing for the “abnormal.”

### Substack is still subpar

The promise of Substack's “Beta Discoverability” was that readers would more easily find new writers on the platform. Additionally, a newsfeed was supposed to launch that helped promote writers and creators to people who didn’t yet follow them. This was supposed to happen in early 2022.

Guess what, neither outcome happened.

Beta discoverability turned out to be nonexistent, or perhaps just made up entirely, while the news feed was just a simple reformatting of the content headers displayed by the creators who users were already following.

The truth about Substack is that it’s great if you show up to the party with an existing e-mail list and a minor celebrity status, but if you’re just starting out, it’s absolute garbage — this didn’t change in 2022.

### NFTs are crashing

According to a top productivity writer, “[2021 was the year NFTs took over the world](https://medium.com/better-humans/how-to-live-an-abnormal-life-in-2022-8f3c8088dcf).” Blockchain jocks love NFTs because they supposedly solved the problem of proving ownership in a system that inherently rejects ownership.

In other words, people who believe in blockchain technology claim to want a decentralized and free internet that isn’t focused on making money and rejects all economic regulation and authority, except for when it comes time to pay the bills. To fix that little problem, someone invented NFTs as a pseudo regulation, that isn’t really a “regulation” because it’s decentralized and without authority, yet it helps people prove ownership of something that doesn’t actually exist.

In other words, NFTs are bullshit.

The very notion of ownership is rooted in the concepts of regulation, legitimacy, and authority. Without some form of governance assigning and protecting ownership, then it simply doesn’t exist — kind of like NFTs.

NFTs really were just a way for the digital cretins to have their cake, and everyone else's cake, and eat it all themselves.

Apparently, 2022 was supposed to be a renaissance year for NFTs in that they would start to apply to written articles, pages of books, book covers as collectables, quotes, and even Tweets.

While there have, indeed, been a few rich idiots who have purchased a Tweet here and there for an obscene amount of money, the NFT market is crashing in spectacular fashion along with its cousin cryptocurrency.

Don’t expect a recovery anytime soon.

### Next up, more positive thinking

The problem with content creators who produce mind-numbing productivity dribble that promises that the future will be better despite the fact that hundreds of millions are struggling to survive day-to-day, is that they give people a drug that makes them think that everything is okay.

However, when society is no longer safe and violence and poverty are the reality for millions and loom for tens of millions more, that’s precisely when we should all be taking a good hard look at what is really going wrong.

Rather than let yourself be duped by slingers of false hope who would have you believe that you are actually a closet positivity warlock who has been held in captivity by your own brainwashed mind that tells you that you aren’t special, and all that is needed is a secret potion of attitude changing to unlock the secret of prosperity, why not instead get on board with the people who have actually proven to predict the future correctly?

This is super dangerous since the volatility of the cryptocurrency markets could therefore spill over into the conventional financial system, causing the prices of the assets that are backing so-called “stablecoins” to fall suddenly, which could subsequently cause a panic that results in a broader sell-off across all financial markets.

Simply put, if cryptocurrencies become popular enough, then they could cause recessions and global shortages in everything from electricity and metals to food and clothing.

But this isn’t news, since it was predicted by many people way before it was clear to the entire world. Today, anyone who doesn’t accept this fact is just in denial, or incredibly stupid.

Neither one of these two clowns has any ideas about how to actually land.

The result of this situation will be bad. Six months from now, unemployment in America will be far higher, crime will have increased even further, people will default on everything from home loans to medical bills to credit cards, and nobody will agree on what caused the crash in the first place.

But I can tell you what caused it.

America will experience extreme financial pain because most people wanted to ignore the bad news, the real news, the facts, and instead, pretend that everything was okay, until it wasn’t.

Make no mistake, what happens next in the world will be just as profound as the pandemic.
