### Finance & Money | Student Loans

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*_MO38r12DM3NQKHt)

Photo by [The New York Public Library](https://unsplash.com/@nypl?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

The U.S. federal government should discharge all $1.9 trillion in loans, and then eliminate all federally backed educational loans and grants. The entire American academic system is predatory and runs off of debt, and the only way to stop it is to wipe the slate clean and then shut it down for good. The debt relief would pay for itself since no more loans would be offered.

Just like everything else, the less than half-trillion-dollar loan forgiveness is controversial. Republicans say it is a woke drag on the economy, while the Dems say it’s necessary to liberate the indebted from unfair and predatory loans.

Both are right.

The real problem with student loans is that federal subsidies have distorted education, and instead facilitated an academic-industrial complex that claims to carry the keys to the kingdom of the American Dream. As a result, all academic institutions in the United States have raised their tuition to a level where virtually no one can afford it without first taking out a massive loan.

This bureaucratic-educational complex has produced millions of people, both young and older, who have tremendous debt yet no marketable skills that many employers are willing to pay for. The other issue is that many skills that were important 10 or 20 years ago, no longer are.

### Expensive education is a scam

We’ve all heard that Americans who earn a college degree make more than those who don’t, and on average, that is true. However, averages don’t work for individuals, and the fact of the matter is that there are millions of individuals saddled with insurmountable debt and no hope of ever getting out from under.

That those people took on this debt in an effort to better themselves and become more productive members of society should be viewed as virtuous, not as a stupid financial decision.

That doesn’t change the reality that those people got screwed.

Today, there are many recent grads with expensive degrees who earn way less than trade labourers without any higher education. Some argue that graduates with “worthless degrees” should just work in the same fields as those without education.

This won’t work.

A worker who earns $45,000 a year and has no monthly payment for a student loan can survive. A worker who earns the same, but has to pay $700 per month to pay back a loan for something that provided no financial value, is in trouble.

This is a conservative example, as the real world for many is actually far worse.

A film graduate from the Tisch School at [New York University](https://collegescorecard.ed.gov/school/fields/?193900-New-York-University), one of the top film schools in the world, earns about $30,000 per year upon graduating, yet they have around $114,000 in debt. PhD’s in the humanities make an average of $55,000, which is far less than the average electrician who never went to college.

Demand for skilled trade workers, such as electricians and machinists, is high, as the labour market has roughly 10 million job openings in the U.S. The vast majority of these jobs don’t require college degrees. While many openings require skills training, federal aid doesn’t typically apply to boot camps and trade schools.

Some argue that even if jobs don’t require a college education, a bachelor’s degree can help employers screen for attributes such as a strong work ethic and a sense of personal responsibility. While that might have once been true, students nowadays don’t show up for class or turn in assignments on time, and then make ridiculous excuses and intimidate instructors to get their way.

In the past, students graduated from university because they completed a rigorous set of tasks and tests that proved they had attained a level of thought and knowledge required for more advanced roles in society.

Today, graduation rates are higher than ever, GPAs as well, yet the competencies of recent graduates are at an all-time low.

### Stop the steal

The bottom line is that colleges and universities have become disconnected from the real economy and instead operate too much like businesses for profit. Enabled by the student loan program, schools grow by charging higher tuition, which allows them to invest in luxuries such as impressive fitness centres or celebrity professors. All of this is meant to attract more students who will pay higher prices.

When students graduate and can’t find a job, what does the academic industry tell them to do?

Go to graduate school.

In [the 2019–20 school year](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/ctb/graduate-degree-fields), universities awarded more than a million graduate degrees — about half as many as bachelor’s degrees.

This was never the case before, as grad school was always reserved for the best performers in four-year universities. Furthermore, there are more master's degrees out there today than ever before, with highly specialized fields that simply shouldn’t require expensive education.

Over the past two decades, [the number of Americans](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022/educational-attainment.html) over 25 with a master’s or doctorate has more than doubled, and today, [half of the $1.6 trillion in federal student debt](https://www.brookings.edu/blog/up-front/2020/10/09/who-owes-the-most-in-student-loans-new-data-from-the-fed/) is now held by Americans with graduate degrees — present company included.

Government loans have been driving too many people to pursue degrees that simply don’t pay. Biden’s loan forgiveness is a huge mistake in that it will only further incentivise this cycle of raising tuition and drawing in more students. Furthermore, loan forgiveness does next to nothing for the millions who have loans that exceed $50,000.

If the American Government discharges all loans and then stops offering new ones, then schools will have no choice but to adjust prices downward, and fast. Perhaps more importantly, students who still want loans will need to go to a private lender who will be far more discerning regarding the ROI of the loan.

The result will be that schools will be required to rationalize why the cost of a certain program is what it is and allocate their resources accordingly.

Further, those seeking loans for trade schools and boot camps might have better options, as the private lending system will mature so that such smaller loans may finally become financially viable.

The solution for America is to give everyone who got screwed a way out, and then never let anyone get screwed again.
