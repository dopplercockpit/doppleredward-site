### What is a microstartup?

Just like so many of the other bullshit concepts in tech and modern investing, there is no real definition for what a *microstartup* is, but of course, they are easily identified by a few bullet point attributes:

- They are usually run by only 1 to 5 people who live on Airbnb.
- They require only a small amount of capital to get started, aka bootstrapping, because they never actually produce anything.
- The goal is to make some kind of revenue, although, it’s terribly unpopular to admit that they are actually out to make money.

Microstartups claim to be setup to scale without the need for large teams and to produce income for its creator, and so the ideas they tackle are always “one major problem that users want to solve,” and of course, are willing to pay money for.

Yet, the concept of microstartups versus the typical tech venture is that micro entrepreneurs convince themselves they aren’t out for money. Money comes by accident, by doing something important.

Got that?

The beauty of running a microstartup is that creators can often work on several at a time, because they are passion projects aimed at improving the world.

In other words, founders convince themselves and others that they aren’t actually working, even though they never ever see their kids.

### Look to solve a problem that doesn’t matter

The goal of the microstartup is to figure out an easy problem that people are willing to pay money for, which of course always means something in software, apps or websites, and that can generate subscriptions and/or ad revenue.

After they scale to a certain point, the idea is to cash in and be acquired.

For example, Wordle, a microstartup that was purchased by New York Times for more than $1 million, claims that their intention, of course, was never to be acquired. However, by accidentally reaching the sales velocity and scale that they did, well… it was just too much to bear, and so they all decided to become overnight millionaires, despite their love for word games.

There are many examples of microstarups who claim not to be out for money, who end up making mountains of money, but they always say that it’s not really the point.

Becoming insanely rich for no reason is just the side effect of doing what they love.

The point is that microstartups are the next nauseating fad that rich, white, mostly male, entitled 20 somethings will use to mine their well-established social network which controls an unbelievable amount of cash that gets thrown into addiction inducing products.
