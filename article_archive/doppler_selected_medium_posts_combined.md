### The American economic model is not capitalism

America is not a pure capitalist society — it is a [*capitalist mixed economy*](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/mixed-economic-system.asp)with strong elements of [*corporatism*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporatism)*.*

The U.S. economic system subscribes to free-market capitalism in that it primarily leaves the means of production in the hands of private ownership, however, the American government enacts many subsidies that change natural supply and demand, and it also runs a number of publicly owned corporations.

Pure capitalist systems do not control the markets as much as the [American government does](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/economics/11/government-regulations.asp).

Who does have a pure capitalist system? Well, despite popular perception, [there is no known historical or modern economy that is pure capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) — all of the existing economic systems in the world currently under democratic governments fall somewhere along the *mixed continuum*.

### Mixed up perceptions

[Capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/020915/what-are-differences-between-capitalism-and-socialism.asp) is an economic system that functions within a market economy, where the state allows for the private ownership of capital goods.

That’s it.

Capitalism depends upon government regulation to assign and protect private ownership — without this regulation, nobody can really own anything.

Millions of Bernie Sanders supporters now openly decry Capitalism as inherently evil and call for the adoption of what he, and now they, label as Democratic Socialism*.*

Democratic Socialism has never in history ever existed in reality — in fact, there has never been a widely adopted economic system that has allowed for capital goods to be either left entirely to private ownership or entirely controlled by a government.

Democratic Capitalism,on the other hand***,*** is an economic system that combinesa liberal democratic political system with a capitalist economic system, therefore, it enables a capitalist economy that is subject to control by a political system that is supported by the majority.

*This* is what Bernie Sanders actually advocates for, *not Socialism.*

Sure, I know, Bernie calls himself a socialist, but in practice and in theory, he definitely is not.

Many economists and journalists have noted that Sanders’s views aren’t really socialist, and rather are based on tax-funded social benefits rather than social ownership of the means of production. Sanders philosophies really are better described as social democracy and social welfare, which are political and social ideologies rather than economic. Sanders is perhaps a believer in reform to capitalism, but he is not a socialist.

[Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin), a true socialist, believed that capitalism bred inequality and uneven economic and political development. Lenin further believed that the path to solving the problem of capitalism was through strong government intervention, which is known as the [dictatorship of the proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_the_proletariat).

> *“I do believe in private companies that thrive and invest and grow in America, companies that create jobs here, rather than companies that are shutting down in America and increasing their profits by exploiting low-wage labor abroad.”*

Both of these quotes by Bernie Sanders shows that he rejects oligarchy, rejects the public ownership of capital goods, and wants the government to create incentives for American owned firms to operate in America, thus keeping the ownership of wealth in America— this is about as Capitalist as you can get.

### Capitalism does not mean Corporatism

A [capitalist system](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) and a [free-market](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/freemarket.asp) system are both economic environments that are based on the law of [supply and demand](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/economics/11/intro-supply-demand.asp), but [they are not the same](https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/042215/what-difference-between-capitalist-system-and-free-market-system.asp) thing. Both are market-driven economies where market factors determine supply and demand.

Both systems utilize a market-based approach to determine the price and production of goods and services, however, the key difference is that [capitalism](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/capitalism.asp) is focused on the *creation* of wealth and ownership, whereas a more pure free-market system is focused on the *exchange* of wealth, goods and services. *A pure free market* system allows for entirely free competition in the economy, without any intervention from outside forces, while capitalismrequiresprivate ownership and government regulation.

Modern [American Libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism) are true supporters of a free-market system absent of capitalism, and thus advocate for the removal of most or all forms of government intervention in the markets.

The existing American-Corporatist focused economic model is essentially a combination of [mercantilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism) and [finance capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_capitalism) that treats a worker like a disposable cost commodity — *this* is what has created the culture of short-term earnings, not capitalism.

An example of short-term earning culture wreaking havoc on society is in the rapid expansion of global firms through mergers and acquisitions (M&A). Investors vote for M&A actions because it provides short-term earnings via the removal of [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost), however, the combining of firms does little to create actual new economic value — the only role capitalism plays here is that private firms and individuals have the ability to own capital goods.

### Productivity | Remote Work Possibilities

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*wCe9I389OxUytuho)

Photo by [Cytonn Photography](https://unsplash.com/es/@cytonn_photography?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

“Overemployment” refers to when people have more than one job. While previously the term referred to working more hours than necessary for financial reasons, many people are now technically over-employed while still working a 40-hour work week. This is becoming increasingly popular since many workers are realizing that they don’t need 40 hours to do their BS “40-hour-per-week job.” People may engage in over-employment to supplement their income or achieve specific financial goals, such as buying a house or saving up for crazy vacations.

In other words, many remote workers and digital nomads have traded the water cooler and surfing the web for more real work that pays real money.

There are several ways people can use over employment to make extra money, including:

- Taking on a part-time job in addition to their full-time job.
- Freelancing or doing gig work in their spare time.
- Starting a side business or monetizing a hobby.
- Renting out their assets, such as a spare room or a car, on sharing economy platforms.
- Offering their skills and services on freelance websites or marketplaces.
- Teaching things to people far far away.
- Providing functional solutions in an area of expertise.

It’s important to note that over-employment can also have serious negative consequences, such as burnout, fatigue, and reduced productivity. It’s also possible to get caught, and the ethics on this are blurry.

It is essential to balance earning extra income with maintaining a healthy work-life balance and ensuring that additional work doesn’t interfere with primary job responsibilities, lest the main gig squeeze finds out about the gig mistresses and asks for a career divorce.

## The Benefits of Overemployment

Over employment can offer many amazing benefits including:

### Extra income

Overemployment allows individuals to earn additional income, which can be used to pay off debt, save for future goals, or improve their overall financial situation. Most people will gladly receive extra money for the amount of work.

### Skill development

Taking on additional work can provide opportunities for individuals to learn new skills or develop existing ones, which can lead to career advancement in the primary gig. Content creators who take on a second job teaching courses on consumer behavior are bound to learn something new.

### Flexibility

Many types of over-employment, such as freelancing or gig work, offer flexibility in terms of when and where work is done, which can be convenient for individuals who have other commitments or responsibilities. This becomes even more interesting when leveraging time zones — I love it when I’m responding to my client's needs at 5 am local time.

### Networking

Over-employment can provide opportunities to meet new people and expand professional networks, which can be beneficial for career growth and development, and lead to more and better-paid normal employment

### Diversification

Having multiple sources of income can provide a sense of security, as it reduces reliance on a single source of income and helps individuals weather financial emergencies or downturns. If the primary gig cheats on you or leaves you, at least you’ll always have your side piece(s).

## How Digital Nomads Use Overemployment

Digital nomads use tech to work remotely while traveling the world or living in locations otherwise unattainable. For example, I live in France in a town that has absolutely nothing to do with my primary job. Over-employment can be a useful strategy for digital nomads to earn extra income and support their travel lifestyle, in several ways:

### Freelance Writing

Many digital nomads work as freelancers, offering their skills and services to clients around the world. Taking on additional freelance work can help them earn extra income and expand their portfolio of clients for future gigs. Writing in English is actually a marketable skill outside of English-speaking countries, so taking on projects of all types is common.

### IT Consulting

With the increasing reliance on technology, IT consultants are in high demand to help companies manage their IT systems, develop software solutions, and optimize their technology infrastructure. People who have functional expertise in a given area might be surprised to discover that they know more about deploying a new SaaS platform than the developers who created it.

### Teaching

Some digital nomads teach English, or teach other subjects online *in English*, which can provide a steady source of income and the flexibility to work from anywhere. This works especially great in Europe for all topics of business including marketing, finance, sales, and of course, IT.

### Affiliate marketing

Digital nomads who have a blog or social media following may be able to earn extra income through affiliate marketing, where they promote products or services and receive a commission for any sales generated. Bloggers can earn money through affiliate marketing, sponsored posts, and advertising — according to a 2021 survey by [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/), the median annual income for bloggers is $11,000, but top earners can make six-figure incomes. Even the Median is a good boost as a side hustle.

YouTube creators can also earn money through advertising revenue, brand deals, and merchandise sales. The highest-earning YouTube creators can make millions of dollars per year, but smaller creators may earn a few hundred dollars per month with just a few hours of work.

Podcasters can earn money through sponsorships, advertising, and merchandise sales. According to a 2021 survey by [Podcast Insights](https://www.podcastinsights.com/), the top 1% of podcasters earn more than $50,000 per episode, while the median podcast earns less than $1,000 per year.

Overall, the amount of extra money content creators can earn via affiliate marketing, depends upon factors such as the type of content they create, the size of their audience, and the platforms they use to monetize their content, but extra money is extra money no matter.

### Business Consulting

Business consultants are also in high demand as companies seek to improve their operations and profitability. Business consultants can provide advice on strategy, marketing, operations, finance, and other areas such as what IT systems would be good to implement, and which are not.

## How to find international consulting jobs where you can work remotely

### Consulting firms

Many consulting firms offer remote work options for their employees. You can visit the websites of consulting firms that interest you and check if they have any remote job openings. Scouring LinkedIn for the smaller and more bite-sized firms is a good idea, since their hiring practices are oftentimes less arduous to navigate, but they still have the capability to secure a healthy budget to pay you with.

### Freelance websites

There are several freelance websites that allow you to create a profile and bid on consulting projects. Some popular freelance websites include Upwork, Freelancer, and Guru.

### Networking

Networking can be a powerful way to find remote consulting jobs. You can attend industry events, connect with professionals on LinkedIn, and join industry-specific groups to build your network. Online job boards and forums can be good places to do this — there are many online job boards and forums that specialize in remote work such as Remote.co, We Work Remotely, and Working Nomads.

## The Dangers of Overemployment

### Burnout

Overemployment can lead to burnout, which is a state of emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion caused by prolonged stress. When someone is over-employed, they may not have enough time for rest and self-care, which can lead to burnout and a decline in physical and mental health.

### Decreased productivity

When someone is over-employed, they may not be able to devote enough time and energy to each job or task, which can lead to decreased productivity and quality of work.

### Strained relationships

Overemployment can take a toll on personal relationships, as the person may not have enough time to spend with friends and family, leading to feelings of isolation and loneliness.

### Legal and financial risks

Taking on too many jobs or side hustles can lead to legal and financial risks, such as tax issues or breaches of contracts. It’s important to research and understands the legal and financial implications of each job or side hustle before accepting it.

### Career stagnation

Overemployment can prevent someone from focusing on their primary career or goals, leading to career stagnation and missed opportunities for growth and advancement.

## Putting it all together

It is very possible for many to take side hustle jobs internationally. With advances in technology and the increasing popularity of remote work, many side hustle jobs can be done from anywhere in the world, as long as you have an internet connection. Regardless of the method you use to find remote consulting jobs, it’s important to carefully review job descriptions, research companies, and tailor your application materials to each opportunity to increase your chances of success.

It’s important to find a balance between work and other aspects of life to avoid the potential dangers of over-employment. This may involve setting boundaries, prioritizing self-care, and being selective about the jobs and side hustles taken on.

### Personal Finance | Cryptocurrency

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*1kHHhFmGUF15HywO)

I grew up in Detroit, the place where raves were first invented during the early 1990s by a group of misfit electronic music enthusiasts who decided to make good use of the city's many abandoned factories. It’s not a good time in Detroit unless there are drugs, loud music, wild lights, trespassing, and violence — raves always promised all of these things. Soon thereafter, the “raver” became a hallmark moniker for people who were dirty and unkempt and over the age of 12 — who abused the drug Ecstacy (MDMA) and sucked on baby pacifiers.

This was the origin of the rave.

Today, this same type of event is the party of choice for crypto bros who attend throwback-style raves in a wealthy and sterile neighbourhood littered with crypto firms.

Of course, I’m talking about the Wynwood district in Miami, Florida, a trendy and expensive part of town known for its many colourful murals, and as being one of the city’s “[most happening districts](https://www.google.com/search?q=Wynwood&oq=Wynwood&aqs=chrome..69i57j46i131i433i512j46i175i199i512l3j0i512l3j46i175i199i512j0i512.336j0j9&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8).” Man-children from all over come to Wynwood to admire the contrived outdoor museum showcasing large-scale works by some of the world’s best-known street artists, converted warehouses now housing craft breweries, and funky freaky deeky art galleries selling all kinds of overpriced wares, including NFT’s.

In Wynwood, everyone is hip and young and frequents the many chic clothing boutiques, stylish bistros and late-night bars that serve craft-artisan-mixology-infused cocktails. The area is home to the headquarters of Blockchain.com, a well-funded crypto-trading platform, and will soon host the American base of “ftx,” an exchange run by Sam Bankman-Fried, a 30-year-old well-known crypto billionaire with really bad hair.

One famous rave in the crypto community was marketed as “raave” to would-be partygoers by Stani Kulechov, the founder of Aave, an open-source protocol through which people are able to lend or borrow various cryptocurrencies. The protocol is one of the more successful decentralised finance (DeFi) applications in the world. His “raave” was a far cry from the traditional raves of 1992 Detroit. Mr Kulechov, a Finn, instead threw a chic party on the sidelines of Devcon, an annual global conference hosted by the Ethereum Foundation, the non-profit organisation that brings together developers working on Ethereum — you know, the second-most valuable blockchain in the world that is trying to centralize decentralization so that crypto won’t be banned by governments.

The party took place in a converted warehouse in Bogata, filled with plants to evoke a jungle theme. The bar served spicy “mezcalitas” (whatever the hell that is) and the music was managed by British DJ Veronika Fleyta.

The toilets worked. There were even taxis waiting outside to take tired “raavers” back to their hotels. No one got shot. Ecstacy may have been present in small doses, but no one was sucking on pacifiers or collapsing on the dancefloor from dehydration.

The crowd included a bubly Colombian girl who had participated in a hackathon before the conference kicked off, a suave French programmer who was working on a method to distribute aid via Ethereum, and a cheerful Mexican guy waiting in line for a “raave” t-shirt, emblazoned with a friendly ghost logo — of course, because crypto bros love ghosts.

While pro cryptos will argue that the party is evidence that crypto is a more mature industry now — despite the fact that it’s nothing more than a ridiculous religion based on the hope that the entire world will abandon traditional money and instead embrace the batshit crazy inventions of a few old teenage boys — reality suggest something different.

Emulating illegal parties held by ravaged and impoverished people in a ruined city and instead turning them into sterile upgrades of cheesy corporate annual events suggests that the people holding the reigns of the crypto world are a bit out of touch. It might be safe to say that they have little understanding of the plight of previous rebels — people who actually suffered for a cause.

For many past misfits, rejecting the system was necessary for survival, not a mode for finding a novel way to buy a pizza, porno, or an overpriced electric vehicle.

During “crypto winter” earlier this year, crypto prices plunged, and yet at Devcon, several firms announced that, despite the horrific crash, they had still managed to raise capital from venture firms.

UniSwap Labs, founded by the creator of UniSwap, another successful DeFi protocol, raised $165m at a $1.7bn valuation. Established venture-capital firms, like Andreessen Horowitz, which participated in the fundraising round, are sticking with crypto. Crypto bros argue that this is a reflection of the fact that the tech is getting better, too. Devcon came on the heels of “the merge,” when Ethereum switched the mechanism by which it adds data to its blockchain. The change essentially eliminated Ethereum’s carbon footprint overnight. The next technological improvements are likely to increase the scale and reduce the cost of transactions, which should make Ethereum useful for more than just financial applications.

But it doesn’t matter, since most crypto raves today are [held in strip clubs](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2022/10/27/the-surprising-maturity-of-the-crypto-rave-crowd). At the end of the day, crypto bros are exactly what they appear to be.

Don’t expect much by way of maturity and enlightenment from the crypto community.

The story of Glenn Frazier is an epic tale of leadership, bravery, perseverance, and heroism. Ironically, the story begins with an act of cowardice that was driven by a lack of self-esteem, followed by an act of anger, and then yet another act of cowardice. In the end, we see how the most extreme circumstances can bring the unlikely misfit to become a paragon of exemplary leadership.

Signing up for the military was an impulsive escape plan after Alabama native Glenn Frazier fled his home town and subsequently angered a shotgun-wielding bar owner. Legally too young to enlist, Frazier waited three months to tell his friends and family where he had gone, rather than risk having his mother call the Army and demand that he be sent home.

Once he was safely on another continent, heartbroken 16-year-old Glenn Frazier sent a letter to his parents, letting them know that he was a member of the 75th Ordinance Batallion in the U.S. Army. He explained to them that everything was going to be fine, and assured them that he was safe in the Philippines.

Glenn Frazier sent that letter in November of 1941.

## An Irresistible Force Meets an Immovable Object

Frazier grew up in Fort Deposit, Alabama, a small town near the capital Montgomery. The star of the town’s high school football and basketball teams, Frazier was raised as a devout Catholic and came from a society where marriage tended to happen early in life.

Despite being mostly well-behaved and god-fearing, Frazier liked to ride motorcycles, and occasionally had a problem with authority. His rebelliousness was not deemed endearing by many, including the mother of Jamie Morris, a local girl that Frazier had taken quite fondly to.

Jamie was everything to Frazier, and also his planned prom date; however, they had not yet declared that they were boyfriend and girlfriend. In 1941 Fort Deposit, going steady was a seriously important step, and it wasn’t uncommon for it to quickly lead to engagement and marriage. Frazier was hopelessly in love and wanted Jamie to be his girlfriend.

What happened next changed young Glenn Frazier’s life forever.

But Glenn Frazier wasn’t the only boy with his sights set on young Jamie. Another young man, 17 years old and living in Nashville, Tennessee, had quickly become Frazier’s rival for Jamie’s hand. To make matters worse, Jamie’s mother wanted to pair her with a boy from Nashville and she was also no fan of Frazier nor his 1936 Harley-Davidson.

Mrs. Morris had arranged for her daughter to visit the boy and his parents in Tennessee, and Jamie was to leave on the train the next morning.

Frazier and Jamie spend their last night together talking, crying and kissing inside a 1933 Chevrolet that was parked on a secluded patch of grass. In those days, when a young woman went to a young man’s house to meet the parents and family, that meant there might be a marriage. So Frazier warned Jamie that if she didn’t stand up to her mother and instead left for Tennessee, he wouldn’t be there when she got back.

He returned Jamie to her home at 3:00 am and was promptly chased off by Mrs. Morris coming after him with a broomstick, shouting out to Frazier to never to come back.

Jamie’s train was scheduled to depart at 6:30 am. Lovesick, wrought with anxiety, and unable to sleep, Frazier took his motorcycle to the train station and hid behind some bushes to watch. What happened next changed young Glenn Frazier’s life forever.

Jamie arrived with her mother, Mrs. Morris, who walked her onto the train and watched as the train left the station. Frazier, feeling betrayed and heartbroken, followed through on his threat and rode his motorcycle to Montgomery.

## Teenage Wasteland

Frazier arrived in Montgomery and went to a local juke joint where he and several of his football friends had developed a reputation for getting into fights with the locals. While many of the patrons drank beer, Frazier kept to a Nickel Coke.

Normally, Frazier was in this bar with many of his friends, but that day he was all alone. Recognizing the young man as a trouble maker, the owner approached Frazier and told him to leave right away. Frazier defiantly replied that he’d leave after he had finished his Coke. The owner snatched it out of his hand and barked at him to leave immediately and never come back.

Heartbroken, emotional, and sleep-deprived, Frazier made it just a short couple of blocks away from the bar before anger consumed him, and led him to do something outrageous.

Back in the juke joint, the owner was again behind the bar when Glenn Frazier came charging through the large entranceway on his motorcycle, causing the swinging doors to violently slam into the bar. Frazier proceeded to ride out onto the dance floor, leaving black rubber tracks everywhere as he performed figure eights and donuts.

Then the owner produced a shotgun, and Frazier hit the gas and sped out the door, turning off the driveway and disappearing into the woods to get out of range as quickly as possible.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*44sTBNS4cJp1hefL-v6eFw.jpeg)

Photo by [Chor Tsang](https://unsplash.com/@chortsang?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/train-station-old?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

Frazier kept riding until he reached a familiar service station where he would frequently hang out with his friends. When Fraizer arrived, the owner had already heard about what happened, and said to Frazier:

> “I know that man. That man is mean. He’s gonna kill you. He knows you live in Fort Deposit and he’s not going to rest until he gets you. But get out of here. I don’t want him shooting you here.”

A few minutes later, his teenage emotions buzzing with both adrenaline and depression, Frazier found himself in front of an Army recruiter’s office.

> “I stood out front for a minute and I thought, this might be a good time to try the Army.” — Glenn Frazier.

It was the summer of 1941 and America was at peace. The news had spoken some of Hitler’s provocations in Europe, but no one in Alabama had worried that it would affect them in any way.

Stubbornly determined to keep his word that he was not going to be there when Jamie returned, and fearful for his life, young Glenn Frazier enlisted.

## Out of the Frying Pan

The day he signed the papers, Frazier was only 16 years old, but the law required that he be at least 21. In 1941, records were not what they are in 2019, and so the young man was able to convince recruiters that he was indeed old enough to sign up. He looked older than 16, and he was riding a motorcycle, both of which probably helped him with his deceit.

The Army offered Frazier assignments in Alaska, the Panama Canal, or the Philippines. Frazier chose the latter because he figured that it was the one place the juke joint owner couldn’t drive to. That afternoon he was on the train headed for Mississippi’s Camp Shelby.

By the time his parents received the letter he had sent them, the United States was at war with Japan.

Four days after arriving at Camp Shelby, Frazier was on a train bound for San Francisco, where he would take a boat to the Philippines, which is where he would go through basic training.

Once he arrived, Frazier was meant to join the 31st Infantry Division, but the boat arrived too late and he missed the start of basic training. As a result, Frazier was moved to the 75th Ordinance Batallion, the division responsible for all ammunition for every military division in the Philippines.

Frazier felt that his job was easy, and Ordnance School, where he learned the ins and outs of the various explosives, was oftentimes fun. He spent most of his days driving big trucks used to move bombs and bullets from large naval vessels as they arrived in the port of Manila.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*ci42zIK2HBwxU4G3_tJz8A.jpeg)

Photo by [Will Porada](https://unsplash.com/@will0629?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/ammunition-wwii?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

Over the next few months, Frazier had made a new best friend, Gerald Block, who was stationed with him at the main base in Manila, also where General Douglas MacArthur’s headquarters were located. Frazier and Block would frequent the entertainment district where they drank alongside Japanese naval sailors.

One night, Frazier and Block got involved in a brawl over the jukebox. The fight was between about twenty Americans and a couple of dozen Japanese sailors. Frazier described the scene as,

> “being like a movie, then we hear the whistle that tells you the MPs are coming, so we start bailing out the windows, this was the second floor mind you, and we shinnied out over that roof and waited until the MPs ran in the building, then we started dropping off and running so they wouldn’t catch us.”

While in Manilla, Frazier also met a new girl, a local Filipina named Nelda. While his heart still ached for Jamie, he convinced himself that his new life was here on the other side of the world. Frazier quickly became close with Nelda’s family as well, including her brother, Bobby, who crafted a Filipino-style hunting knife made custom for his new friend and potential brother-in-law.

Meanwhile, back in Fort Deposit, Jamie Morris was waiting for Frazier to return so that she could tell him that she had chosen him, and wanted to marry him. She had not followed her mother’s demands and was not in love with the boy from Tennessee. For Jamie, Glenn Frazier was the one.

## Into the Fire

Glenn Frazier turned 17 on December 1st, 1941. Frazier’s company was scheduled to be sent to other airbases in the Pacific later that week, and young Glenn was preparing for travel. Plans quickly changed, however.

By the time his parents received the letter he had sent them, the United States was at war with Japan.

On the morning of Sunday, December 7th, the Japanese fleet launched a massive attack on the naval base in Pearl Harbor, Hawaii. General MacArthur froze and did nothing to prepare after learning of the attack at Pearl Harbor. Hours later, Japanese planes bombed Corregidor Island, just across the bay from Manila. This was just the beginning of a full assault against the Americans in Philipinnes. By morning, approximately 6,000 Americans and Filipinos had been killed, and the Japanese had successfully destroyed multiple targets around the islands.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*DwEbDeypB_WZrlUq6AgqPA.jpeg)

Photo by [Thomas Park](https://unsplash.com/@thomascpark?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/ammunition-wwii?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

The commanders in charge in Pearl Harbor, where 2,500 lives were lost, were relieved of duty for falling victim to a sneak attack. There was never any formal reprimand for MacArthur, however, even though he managed to be caught unprepared despite hours of advance warning.

What happened in the Phillippines was a disaster. Most of the American airpower was destroyed that first day, as dozens of fighters and powerful B-17 bombers were blown up on the runway at each of a half-dozen airbases. At one base, Japanese fighter planes were actually able to strafe the airfield with machine-gun fire for 30 minutes, destroying every single plane.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*iZIdxrDXILwxltchpdZWrQ.jpeg)

Photo by [Gary Wann](https://unsplash.com/@ruyton?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/b-17?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

The Philippines were considered lost almost immediately after the initial Japanese attack, and with almost all of the planes destroyed, there was no way to get the thousands of bombs that Frazier and company had spent months storing in warehouses safely to Australia, or onto targets.

Young Glenn Frazier, not yet old enough to vote, had run away because of heartache and escaped a shotgun-wielding bar owner by moving overseas. Now, the 17-year-old found himself in the crosshairs of the Japanese Empire.

## The Spirit to Kill

During the first day of battle, Frazier’s company were ordered to secure as much ammunition as possible from the bases deep in the interior of the island. The first bases they were focused on were on what was knowns as the Bataan peninsula.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*kLboYUONyn8KVkHc7UvPHA.jpeg)

Photo by [Lawrence Hookham](https://unsplash.com/@hookie1001?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/world-war-2?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

For Frazier, killing was against God’s commandments, but during one of the trips onto the peninsula, Frazier, along with a fellow soldier named Lou, was attacked by two Japanese fighter planes. After bombing a hospital, the Japanese fighters saw the two on the road below and went after them. Frazier jumped into a ditch on one side of the road, and his friend Lou jumped into the ditch on the other side. One of the fighters dropped a small bomb, and it landed directly on Lou.

Frazier himself was hit in the leg by a small piece of shrapnel, but Lou laid in pieces scattered about the ditch. Frazier took Lou’s left foot to show to their sergeant as proof that his friend had been killed. This was the first time Glenn Frazier had seen death up close and personal, but it would not be the last.

Lou’s death is what Frazier attributed to a change inside of him. The innocent catholic boy from Fort Deposit now believed he needed to murder as many Japanese as he could get his hands on. Frazier would later say that it was that moment that “God gave me the spirit to kill.”

## No Escape

The killing would begin in earnest about a week later when the first Japanese soldiers began invading the island. Though there were more than 80,000 troops under American command in the Philippines, they were quickly outmaneuvered and forced to retreat to the Bataan peninsula. Many of the soldiers led by General MacArthur were Filipinos that had only received four or five days of training. Because of their association with the locally raised soldiers, Frazier and his best friend Gerald Block knew first-hand the abysmal state of the Filipino troops.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*aTbBZmaDiRBNrnaNclDE6A.jpeg)

Photo by [Duncan Kidd](https://unsplash.com/@we_the_royal?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/ww2?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

The result of the battle in the Philippines would prove to be the worst defeat ever suffered by the U.S. military. Doomed from the start, American forces retreated and were quickly hemmed in on the Bataan peninsula, where they were cut off from most of their supplies, including food and ammo. Frazier and his fellow drivers in the 75th Ordnance were in high demand delivering bullets to the front lines, but they were also involved in constant firefights with Japanese patrols trying to block them from resupplying the troops.

Frazier and Block used their position as supply runners to set up traps for the Japanese soldiers, killing as many as they could. The two teenage boys would tell double agents working with the Japanese that there were supplies of food or ammunition in train cars, or hiding in an empty unattended building. When the Japanese troops arrived to retrieve the supplies, they would unknowingly trigger booby traps that set off large caches of explosive ammunition, killing everyone in proximity.

While the American troops at Bataan held out for help, there would be no reinforcements coming. All of the ships that would have carried a rescuing force were sitting on the seafloor in Pearl Harbor. As time passed, the state of the U.S. forces began to deteriorate, and with limited rations, ammunition, and no air support, the superior Japanese forces were quickly whittling away at the last defenses.

A pivotal battle occurred at a place called Aglaloma Point. MacArthur had retreated to a neighboring island after the land invasion began, but now his outpost was under threat of being overrun. The Navy and Marines had been able to kill many advancing Japanese soldiers in the water, but they were unaware that behind the initial attack were another 5,000 highly trained troops ready to advance and cut the forces in two, thus taking Bataan and neighboring Corregidor, where MacArthur was holed up.

Frazier happened to be driving by Aglaloma Point delivering ammunition to the front lines when an Army Major flagged his Ordnance truck down. The young man from Fort Deposit had the authority to ignore this major, but something inside compelled him to stop.

The major guided Frazier over to a nearby cliff and told him to look below. There, about 500 feet below the two young American men, was a massive force of Japanese soldiers, concentrated on a horseshoe-shaped cove.

The Major explained that there was no way to hit the soldiers below because the cliffs were shielding them from gunfire and that it looked like the troops were preparing to climb the cliffs. Frazier devised a method for using the bombs in his truck, essentially recreating what would happen if they were being dropped from an airplane. The Americans managed to detonate two truckloads of ammunition meant to be dropped from the sky, killing almost the entire force of 5,000 Japanese soldiers.

Those who survived, however, scaled the cliffs and began attacking. Glenn Frazier found himself engaged in savage hand-to-hand combat with soldiers as young as he. Thanks to the knife that Bobby had given him, Frazier emerged from that day a blood-drenched hero of the American military. He had also become a hardened killer.

Despite their successes at Aglaloma Point, MacArthur had retreated from his stronghold and was on a speedboat headed for another island south of the Philippines. He took with him his staff, mother, son, and everything else, and ultimately went to Australia. Frazier and the remaining Americans at Bataan would hold out for another two months before the leadership finally gave the order to surrender. Frazier and his fellow soldiers laid down their guns, unaware of the horrors that were to follow.

## The Bataan Death March

Glenn Frazier and Gerald Block were captured together, stripped naked, and forced to watch as many of their fellow soldiers were shot and beheaded.

Later that afternoon, they were ordered to start marching.

Along with somewhere between 40,000 and 80,000 other Filipino and American soldiers, and with his friend Gerald Block right behind him, Frazier marched for six days and seven nights, without food or water. During one of the days, Frazier and Block were pulled off the line to fill in a bomb crater using shovels. To their horror, they watched as Japanese soldiers pushed exhausted American and Filipino captives into the pit. When the prisoners were no longer of any use, they buried them alive.

> “I marched six days, seven nights. No food. No water. It was about 90 miles total, really, because we had marched 15 miles earlier. We walked together all the way to the last night. The last night, I lost Gerald. He got ahead of me a little bit. I couldn’t even pick up my feet. I had to slide my feet.”

When they finally made it to an old run-down Filipino base called Camp O’Donnell, Frazier collapsed into a patch of grass. Block had been waiting for him with water, and the two spent the next three days nursing each other back to some semblance of health.

More than 18,000 Americans and Filipinos died during the Bataan Death March, and more than 30,000 POWs would die in Camp O’Donnell the coming months.

## Never Give Up

Back in Fort Deposit, Jamie was still holding out hope for the young Fraizer. No one in the small Alabama town had any idea of the horrors that their star athlete was facing at the hands of his captors. Letters began to pour into the town informing mothers that their sons had been killed in action. No letter had yet come to Frazier house.

Meanwhile, at Camp O’Donnel, Frazier and Block withstood the putrid stench of death as thousands of men died of dehydration and various wounds after the forced march. Bodies went rank quickly in the tropical heat, and within the first two weeks, as many as 70 prisoners were dying each day.

Conditions at Camp O’Donnell were primitive, as the POWs lived in bamboo huts, slept on dirt floors, and oftentimes huts didn’t have proper coverage. There was no plumbing, and water was scarce. Mosquitos spread malaria like wildfire, which only compounded the problem of rampant dysentery.

Medicine was in short supply. Food consisted of nothing more than a small ration of rice and vegetable soup, occasionally with scraps of [water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_buffalo) meat. Vitamin deficiency illnesses such as [beriberi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beri-beri) and [pellagra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelagra) developed among many. The Japanese refused most offers of assistance for the POWs, including from the Philippine [Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Cross).

Finding a sufficient number of able-bodied men among the prisoners to bury the dead was not the least of the problems with which the camp authorities were confronted. It was not unusual to have several of the burial detail drop dead from exhaustion and overwork in the midst of their duties, and be thrown into the common grave which they were digging for their dead comrades. Not infrequently men who had collapsed from exhaustion were buried before they were even dead.

Frazier and his friend Gerald were put to work carrying the dead and putting them into mass graves.

Unsure of what fate awaited him, the young Glenn Frazier threw one of his two sets of dog tags into one of the mass graves. He figured that if he was never found, that perhaps his family would get peace if they believed that he died at Camp O’Donnell.

## Never Say Die

After being inseparable for half a year before the war, then enduring combat together, the Death March, and surviving Camp O’Donnell, Frazier and Block were finally separated after three weeks at the camp.

Block was transferred to another POW camp and placed aboard a [Japanese Hell Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_hell_ships) bound for the main islands of Japan.

Frazier’s troubles would continue, as he’d face continued torture and beatings, and several brushes with death which included gangrene, double pneumonia, a standoff with a homicidal Japanese officer, and being locked in a dark pit for ten days.

Frazier ultimately survived all of it and was transferred to Osaka on mainland Japan, where he was placed in a slave labor camp working for the Japanese. During his time there, he and the other American captives worked tirelessly to sabotage Japanese ammunition, equipment, and other supplies.

In a factory where the Japanese made the graphite batteries for their one-man Kamikaze submarine torpedoes, Frazier’s crew learned the markings for good batteries versus bad ones and began switching them, sending nothing but defective batteries out for use aboard the subs, perhaps saving hundreds of American lives.

Working for months on a drydock, they staged repeated fake fights with each other during which time other members of the prison crew would dump rocks and junk into freshly poured concrete to weaken the doors of the new dry dock, which failed when the Japanese finally tested it. The resulting landslide destroyed a submarine and killed several guards.

Frazier and company further spent weeks slowly maneuvering a huge boulder up a hill next to a giant cement mixer, and slipped it in the hopper when the guards were distracted by one of the fake fights. Two weeks later, the boulder finally dropped into the mixer and destroyed it. This set back the Japanese Naval effort by weeks.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*JPees4-saAvIIdo-lFL5cA.jpeg)

Photo by [Chandler Cruttenden](https://unsplash.com/@chanphoto?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/wwii?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

After three years in the prison camps, American bombers started showing up, attacking the port towns where Frazier was located. As things turned for the worse for the Japanese war effort, Japanese officers began making plans to execute all of the POWs in order to hide their war crimes. One day, Frazier and many other prisoners were ordered to dig their own grave and wait around until the guards had received the verdict about what to do with them.

The order to kill the prisoners never came, and instead, one day a courier arrived and delivered the message that Japan had surrendered, and that the war was finally over.

Many Japanese soldiers fled, while others stopped caring. Rather than wait around and see what happened, Frazier and about twenty others took the opportunity to escape and headed for General MacArthur’s new HQ in Japan. Once they arrived, they were promptly sent to a military hospital.

## Back at the Rail Station

By the time Glenn Frazier was released from the Army, he had gained back most of the weight he had lost. Frazier first had a stop in Manilla, where he would connect to another flight bound for San Francisco. Once there he was greeted by Bobby, who informed him that almost his entire family, including Nelda, had been killed.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*35r0qXpst789zwJ-xSQGHg.jpeg)

Photo by [Suzy Brooks](https://unsplash.com/@simplysuzy?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/world-war-2?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

Just over two weeks later Glenn Frazier arrived at Pier 7 in San Francisco, the first time he’d set foot on U.S. soil in five years.

As was the protocol for POWs, the now battle-hardened war veteran Frazier was sent to a hospital, where he learned that his dog tags had indeed been discovered in that mass grave. Glenn Frazier’s family had been informed six months earlier hat he was dead, and so had Jamie Morris. When he called to tell his family otherwise, both his sisters and their mother fainted.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*H1YUZh0uFH__q-oeqxQ4rQ.jpeg)

Photo by [Robert McLay](https://unsplash.com/@robmclay?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/san-francisco-wwii?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

Two weeks later, Frazier was picked up at the same rail station where he had his heart broken. His cousin had taken his motorcycle, and he retrieved Frazier from the station on that very same bike. Frazier actually arrived at the family home in Fort Deposit on the very same day that his brother returned from the European theatre, where he too had been missing, but for only four months.

Jamie, the girl that caused him to run off in the first place, was actually due to be married the day after he returned, but not to the boy from Tennessee. Frazier visited with Jamie, and she explained how she had waited for him, but then gave up hope years later. Frazier quickly forgave her.

Glenn Frazier went on to be married twice, and have several children of his own. He spent the rest of his career in the military and retired with the rank of Colonel. After finally saying farewell to the life of a soldier, [Glenn wrote a book about his experiences during World War II](https://www.colonelfrazier.com/the-book) and trying to adjust to life in America afterward.

The story of Glenn Frazier is indeed tragic, but had it not been for the series of unfortunate events that led him into that hellish situation, he would not have been where he was needed in order to change the world. Frazier, and people like Frazier, were forced to endure years of terror and torture, and yet played a pivotal role in shaping the outcome of history’s most decisive war.

The geopolitical landscape of today and the prosperity resulting from it exists because of unlikely fighters such as Glenn Frazier. Even after the war, Frazier went on to speak the unpopular truth about General MacArther, and of how the United States ultimately abandoned soldiers on Bataan. The story of Glenn Frazier does not support a political ideology — instead, it supports a leadership ideology.

Glenn Frazier never passed up an opportunity to help one of his fellows, regardless of the risk. When given the chance to weaken his enemy, he took it, despite the reality that it could have meant the end of his life. Frazier admitted that he was always terrified when facing his enemy.

Glenn Frazier lived to be 94 years old and leaves behind him a legacy of perseverance and fortitude that can be an example for everyone. While being brave might have prevented Frazier from experiencing the horrors of the Pacific War in the first place, being scared turned him into a hero.

### Financial Crime | Cryptocurrency Crash

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*Rx9bhtuXwzaK1zZGwEcJwg.png)

Photo by [Kristy Pargeter](http://www.kjpargeterimages.co.uk/)

New Jersey-based [Celsius Network LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius_Network), a significant player in cryptocurrency lending, filed for bankruptcy on July 13, 2022, in the Southern District of New York, a mere one month after blocking all customer withdrawals. This occurred one week after the bankruptcy filing of cryptocurrency competitor, [Voyager Digital Holdings Inc.](https://www.investvoyager.com/investorrelations/overview/)

Interestingly enough, both companies filed in the exact same court, and both have locked up literally billions of dollars worth of assets belonging to their customers.

Celsius was founded in 2017 by famed and self-righteous tech entrepreneur [Alex Mashinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Mashinsky), serial angel investor [S. Daniel Leon](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sdanielleon/), and some guy who goes by the name of [“Nuke” Goldstein.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nukegold/)

Celsius and Voyager Digital are both [cryptocurrency exchange](https://www.investopedia.com/best-crypto-exchanges-5071855) companies that loan people real money (aka fiat currency) in exchange for them parking their precious digital currencies onto company-controlled digital servers.

Celsius, one of the world’s largest global cryptocurrency platforms, has positioned itself as a digital platform where customers can “buy, swap, earn, borrow and send crypto — all in one app, with zero fees.” In short, Celsius was a darling of the blockchain world and promised to finally deliver the revolutionary experience that so many crypto enthusiasts have been eagerly anticipating.

Unfortunately for the customers of Celsius, [the company suddenly froze all customer withdrawals and lending activities, including for corporate customers](https://www.wsj.com/articles/celsius-defends-decision-to-halt-withdrawals-at-debut-bankruptcy-hearing-11658189510), and subsequently went belly up.

Why?

### It’s getting hot in here

Celsius was officially launched in 2018 and managed to quickly open $50 million worth of customer accounts. By May of 2019, customers had transferred over $200 million into their Celsius accounts; less than two years later, that number had grown to more than $10 billion.

As a result of this insane growth, in May of 2022, Celsius raised an additional $690 million, at a company valuation of $3 billion.

If this seems fast to you, it’s because it’s almost unheard of and defies the very laws of financial physics.

So why did Celsius grow so fast?

The primary reason is that Celsius claimed to have solved a major problem for cryptocurrency enthusiasts — liquidity.

Despite many creative attempts at making it more liquid, cryptocurrency has remained very difficult to exchange with other assets and fiat currencies. The founders of Celsius believed that crypto asset holders needed a way to convert their cryptocurrency assets into useable currency, aka real money, and that the best way to do this was to set up a platform where crypto users and investors could find each other and make exchanges.

Essentially, Celsius wanted to be an online market exchange.

While that model seemed neat enough, the founders of Celsius wanted more, and so they figured that they could entice crypto users who would jump at the chance to generate yields off their existing holdings, similar to how a savings account at a bank works.

Simply put, people with Celsius accounts could transfer their crypto assets to other Celsius users in exchange for fiat currency, **or** (and this is a big or), they could lend their crypto assets directly to Celsius itself for yearly interest.

Got that?

To sweeten the deal, customers could opt to have Celsius pay the interest in other cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Etherium. When customers chose this interest-generating option, they would receive [CEL tokens](https://celsius.network/cel-token-explained), which if you haven’t already figured out, stands for “Celsius.”

I know what you’re asking yourself, “so could users exchange the CEL Tokens on other exchanges?” And yes, if that’s what you were wondering, they could! There is, indeed, a secondary market for CEL tokens.

Now, if you’re thinking to yourself, “but that’s crazy because then the value of CEL could be affected by outside market influences so my interest won’t be guaranteed,” then you obviously paid attention in kindergarten.

Unfortunately, the cryptocurrency world is loaded with people who subscribe to the rocket-ship-moon-emoji culture, and so they tend to jump at any creative ideas that promise to squeeze a bit more value out of something that doesn’t actually exist in the first place.

It gets worse.

CEL Tokens entitled their holders to certain perks on the platform and effectively functioned as debtor notes that also offered a weird type of loyalty and rewards program, which in turn provided certain customers with highly favourable rates.

Which customers? We’ll get to that in a moment.

Simply put, CEL Tokens were issued in order to reduce the liability that Celsius had to its customers, and so they incentivised customers to take CEL Tokens by making the tokens a tradable asset on digital currency exchanges, and further by offering loyalty rewards in the form of better interest rates, which in turn would likely be used to issue more CEL Tokens.

If that doesn’t make sense to you, don't worry, it’s because you aren’t crazy.

If it *does* make sense, then you probably also know that incentivizing customers to take on a bogus asset in place of actual money in an effort to avoid having to pay your liabilities all at once is, what we call, a [Ponzi Scheme](https://www.investor.gov/protect-your-investments/fraud/types-fraud/ponzi-scheme#:~:text=A%20Ponzi%20scheme%20is%20an,do%20not%20invest%20the%20money.).

### Promises Promises

Of course, the lawsuits are piling up and social media is illuminated with shocked and outraged crypto users, all of whom have been warned time and time again of the dangers of navigating within unregulated markets.

Regardless of what is good and right and decent, in the end, Celsius might actually win all of the lawsuits against it, because it engineered an audacious and blatantly criminal Terms of Use agreement that clearly states that customers aren’t actually customers, they are investors.

Yup.

Celsius very clearly stated in their filing in New York that upon utilizing the rewards programs, users transferred “all right and title” to their crypto assets to Celsius and basically gave Celsius carte blanche to do with those assets what they pleased.

It gets worse when you read [the language from Celsius’ Terms of Use](https://substack.com/redirect/bb428191-0f51-4a67-8f5c-6245ac9435b1?u=6384750):

> In consideration for the Rewards payable to you on the Eligible Digital Assets using the Earn Service, for us entering into any Loan Agreement, and the use of our Services, you grant Celsius, subject to applicable law and for the duration of the period during which you elect to utilize the Eligible Digital Assets in the Earn Service (if available to you) and thus loan such Eligible Digital Assets to us through your Celsius Account, or as collateral under the Borrow Service (if available to you), all right and title to such Eligible Digital Assets, including ownership rights, and the right, without further notice to you, to hold such Digital Assets in Celsius’ own Virtual Wallet or elsewhere, and to pledge, re-pledge, hypothecate, rehypothecate, sell, lend, or otherwise transfer or use any amount of such Digital Assets, separately or together with other property, with all attendant rights of ownership, and for any period of time, and without retaining in Celsius’ possession and/or control a like amount of Digital Assets or any other monies or assets, and to use or invest such Digital Assets in Celsius’ full discretion. You acknowledge that with respect to Digital Assets used by Celsius pursuant to this paragraph:

> You will not be able to exercise rights of ownership;

> Celsius may receive compensation in connection with lending or otherwise using Digital Assets in its business to which you have no claim or entitlement; and

> In the event that Celsius becomes bankrupt, enters liquidation or is otherwise unable to repay its obligations, any Eligible Digital Assets used in the Earn Service or as collateral under the Borrow Service may not be recoverable, and you may not have any legal remedies or rights in connection with Celsius’ obligations to you other than your rights as a creditor of Celsius under any applicable laws.

Now that’s some fine print for ages.

In short, this wordy agreement says that customers don’t own any of the assets that exist within their Celsius accounts — Celsius actually got their customers to give up ownership of anything they put into their accounts.

If you are getting angry right now, that means you understood.

Imagine a bank telling you that once you deposit your money into a checking account, it’s no longer yours. Better yet, imagine next the bank tells you that they will reward you the longer you keep that money in your account because they are going to use it as collateral to take other loans which they will use to juice sick yields off of other investments that you won’t ever benefit from.

This is essentially what Celsius did, but it’s worse because, unlike a typical bank, Celsius actually incentivised users to put all of their different types of digital assets into Celsius accounts.

Remember the rewards and loyalty programs?

When Celsius saw an opportunity to profit by exchanging one currency for another, they would quickly jack up the rewards for that currency on the customer accounts so that people would deposit more of that currency and retain existing assets of said currency into their Celsius accounts.

Unbeknownst to the customer, since the asset was now legally the property of Celsius (per the Terms Of Use agreement), they would flip that currency to realize a fast profit.

For example, imagine that Celsius saw that they could swap [Dogecoin](https://dogecoin.com/) for USD (U.S. Dollar) at a favourable rate, and so they offered incentives to customers to deposit all their Dogecoin into Celsius accounts. Next, Celsius makes the trade and profits big time — simple. The problem with this is that now Celsius customers can’t easily withdraw Dogecoin, because Celsius no longer has any, since they traded it for USD.

In traditional banks, this practice is highly illegal, but in the cryptocurrency world, anything goes.

It gets worse — each time Celsius did this, the customer rewards would remain in place until Celsius could replenish their supply of the cryptocurrency in the event of a demand for withdrawals, thus inflating the value of the currency, even though supply didn’t exist.

Unfortunately for customers, this business model depended upon the entire crypto world not disintegrating overnight, which we all know actually happened very recently.

Voyager Digital followed a similar business model as Celsius and thus was brought to its knees in a similar way. What Voyager Digital did not have, however, was the concept that they outright owned any assets that customers dared deposit into their accounts, and this is probably why Voyager became insolvent before Celsius.

Honesty and ethics can certainly be a bitch.

### Blame it on the other guy

Both Voyager Digital and Celsius stole money from unsuspecting customers after lying to them about the integrity of their respective companies, and both firms promised their users that they would be able to access their assets when needed, similar to a real bank. Despite that, both companies quickly froze all customer accounts when the companies themselves faced insolvency, and both blamed each other for their woes.

In other words, rather than lose their business, they opted to fuck their customers out of all of the assets that they promised to protect, and rather than own up to their mistakes, they opted to blame the world and their competitors.

The reality is that both of these companies operated like criminal syndicates, and that is why they failed.

Celsius essentially turned its customers into unwilling and unsecured creditors, while Voyager Digital just simply lied about users being able to withdraw money at any time, “no questions asked.”

Regardless, both CEOs appear to have no shame.

> The onset of the “crypto winter” combined with the well-publicized collapse of Luna and the failure of several crypto funds/exchanges led to growing industry-wide reluctance to do business with companies, such as Celsius, that held crypto assets. This reluctance was exacerbated by a series of negative media and social media comments about Celsius, a number of which were unsupported and misleading. As a result of all of these factors, users began withdrawing crypto from Celsius’ platform in large amounts and at a rapid pace.

All of this is untrue and dismisses the real reason why the business failed — Celsius was extremely over-leveraged in basically every possible way.

For example, only 27% of Celsius’ ETH1 (Ethereum) holding was liquid, meaning that, on any given day, they could have at best paid only 1 out of 4 of their customers the full balance of Ethereum currency in their existing accounts. Ethereum was, indeed, one of the largest cryptocurrency holdings on the platform.

In other words, Celsius opened a bunch of loans, and then took user deposits and traded them for cryptocurrency, which resulted in them owing a lot of money and not having the reserves to pay those loans back.

Simply put, Celsius is insolvent, and always has been — but don’t believe me, just look at their Balance Sheet as of one week ago:

### Broke again, but not as broke as the customer

Today, Celsius continues to freeze all accounts and activity, and its customers will likely lose everything.

In any event, as you can see, customers entrusted the Celsius platform, as well as Voyager Digital, with a wealth of assets. With wealth comes power and responsibility, and it appears that, in no uncertain terms, both Voyager Digital and Celsius brazenly shirked that responsibility.

> A luxury watch that has smartwatch functionality is just that, a luxurious apparel item that has been augmented to have additional functionality, but at the core, it remains a traditional luxury watch.

Innovation coming out of Silicon Valley has resulted in major global disruptions to many industries that were previously considered impregnable. Automotive, telecommunications, finance, music, mass media, and education have all been wildly altered as a result of the seemingly steady stream of life-altering technology flowing out of Northern California. Despite this, the luxury goods industry had gone largely unaffected, with the more conservative aspects of jewelry and accessories going largely unchallenged.

That all changed with the launch of the Apple Watch, an event that marked the first time that this luxury category was directly threatened by the technological giants of Silicon Valley.

While part of the traditional luxury watch industry was quick to react to this potential disruption from Apple, many traditionalists were just as quick to dismiss the digital revolution of traditional watches as nonsense. Skeptics of the supposed *digital watch revolution* claimed that traditional luxury watches and digital smartwatches are inherently incompatible, and thus the tradition of fine watchmaking craftsmanship is irreplaceable. On the other hand, many European watchmakers took the threat seriously and charted a new course to keep their business relevant.

[Photo by Colleen at Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/photos/watch-wrist-wristwatch-gold-band-166854/)

The debate on the future and strategy of the luxury watch continues today, and whoever gets it right will likely stand to profit greatly, while those who choose the wrong path risk becoming irrelevant.

### The Clocktower & The Microchip

In 2015, the first Apple Watch was released. It was a digital timepiece that was neither mechanical nor the typical quartz electronic device, and the new timekeeper was much more than just a digital time display, as it provided its owner with an entirely new world of functionalities, previously only seen in sci-fi fantasy or comic books. Apple dubbed this new device a *smartwatch*, a nod to the smartphone, its most successful product at the time.

This new high-tech apparel quickly spawned a new debate across the entire watch industry and raised many questions about the future of what was previously perceived to be *timeless* devices.

While many watch producers found shelter in the high price of the Apple Watch, many traditional luxury watchmakers began to question the future of their products, as their watches were often far more expensive than even the Apple Watch. Thus began the debate if to whether or not the luxury watch would be next to be rendered obsolete by the technological revolution.

### Would You Like A Side of Luxury With That?

The big questions today for luxury watchmakers are, will many traditional luxury watch brands be greatly affected by digital disruption, and if so, which brands, why, and most importantly, when?

To obtain the answer to these questions, we can look at the semiotics and luxury codes that shape and define luxury goods, particularly luxury watches.

Luxury codes are pretty straightforward and identify the core elements for every single luxury brand. The codes themselves are based on the notions of *timelessness*, *customers as believers*, *authenticity*, and *heritage*.

When it comes to luxury watches, semiotics help us understand that the watch as an object will be perceived as either hedonistic, symbolic, or statutory.

> Even young brands must link themselves to a commonly shared heritage

Also of importance is that all luxury watches fall into four different categories regarding the functionality of the device. The four functional categories are the *companion watch*, the *legacy watch*, the *tradition watch*, and the *accomplishment watch*.

All of this can seem a bit technical, but understanding these mechanisms behind customer purchasing decisions of luxury watches is what allows us to predict whether or not digital technology can alter those mechanisms.

### A Time For Pieces

T[he luxury industry in general only produces goods and services that are not essential for survival, and as a result, the luxury industry is vulnerable to social and cultural trends in society and, therefore, must respond accordingly in order to stay competitive](https://www.emerald.com/insight/search?q=Patrick%20Duquesne).

In the contemporary luxury goods business, there is a growing awareness among eco-minded customers who care about the impact of their choices upon the environment and society. Many customers have come to expect products that are environmentally friendly, and meet minimum ethical requirements. Luxury companies, therefore, have a motive to be concerned about their perceived impact on society and have a warrant for devoting resources towards what their customers deem important.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*2TR9eJ7k1nbb-EFolkFiTw.jpeg)

Photo by [Alexey Sukhariev](https://unsplash.com/@alexey_sukhariev?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/s/photos/luxury-watches?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

In a [study published in 2017](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01472038/document), it was determined that by analyzing the type of narratives that were being used during customer interviews in regards to their own luxury watches, three different semiotic approaches could be easily distinguished. Many of the respondents focused on the objective characteristics of their timepiece such as rarity, mechanics, and origins. Others, however, tended to explain more about the temporal context on which the watch depended.

In other words, some customers tended to consider the tangible qualities of the watch, while others were more aware of the social appropriateness such as wearing it to a wedding, a nice dinner, a business event, or other social gatherings.

Essentially, the study found that many respondents placed a high value on the social dimension of their watch, and greatly considered what class and category of people were associated with a specific brand and style of watch. Perhaps most important was that the study captured the semiotic temporal ideologies that customers were considering when receiving the various messages and symbols of luxury watches.

In other words, the study captured the emotional motivations for why people spend a lot of money on watches.

### Without a Story, It’s Just Expensive Metal

In order to be legitimate, luxury products must always impart a sense of timelessness. This sense of timelessness is typically embodied by the design of the luxury product itself, but also in the way that a brand communicates about its universe and vision.

In the case of luxury watchmaking, brands must routinely communicate about their history and heritage, as this creates a sense of continuity and durability. More importantly, this connection to history sends the message to the customer that they are a legitimate luxury watchmaker. This doesn’t necessarily mean that in order to be successful, a luxury watch brand must have existed for a long time. It does, however, mean that even young brands must link themselves to a commonly shared heritage.

> A luxury watch must be something more than just another industrial product, otherwise it is simply not legitimate in the luxury world.

An example of this connection is easily seen in the label that states *Swiss Made* on any watch. If a luxury watch brand uses this label, then it means that it is adhering to a particular quality and precision that exists within the broader Swiss watchmaking tradition, regardless of the actual age of the brand itself. Therefore, a young brand claiming that it is Swiss-made positions itself not as a pioneer of this heritage, but as an ardent follower of those shared values.

This concept is important for new entrants in the luxury watch space to attain legitimacy within the overall category, while still being able to stand out on their own as a young luxury watch brand. However, it is important that a young brand be careful about how they employ this communication strategy, since simply claiming heritage does not provide effective differentiation from competitors. For this reason, young brands often find themselves taking a unique approach for providing the link to tradition and heritage.

[Photo by mauriciodss on Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/users/mauriciodss-2387473/)

We can observe this marketing approach in the brands of [HYT](https://www.hytwatches.com/) and [Bell & Ross](https://www.bellross.com/fr?gclid=CjwKCAjw26H3BRB2EiwAy32zhWBhqdI5Brw-sGK3V6xhb88I4jfu1XGPm0iujjo3rvDinlqu91wLsRoC_akQAvD_BwE). In the case of HYT, the brand was launched in 2012 and today is selling watches at prices starting from $25,000. HYT has no historical heritage of its own, however, it has managed to create its own philosophy about what it calls *fluid time*, and though this has been connecting to the historical heritage of general watchmaking. Bell & Ross was first created during1992 in Paris, however, in order to obtain the label *Swiss Made,* the company now produces its watches in La Chaux de Fond, a town on the Swiss side of the French/Swiss border. The watches could just as easily be made on the French side, however, the brand would not be able to claim itself as a Swiss-made watchmaker.

The world of luxury is characterized by the connection of the brand product to tradition and heritage, the brand personality, and brand communication.

One luxury code that is particularly well represented in the luxury watch industry is that the final customer is more than just a consumer, and is an admirer, a follower, and a fan. That a watch brand customer is willing to spend more than $10,000 for a steel watch indeed suggests devotion to a set of shared values. The world of high-end watchmaking is still reserved for experts and enthusiasts, as it is an exclusive and relatively closed environment with strong barriers to entry.

### Electricity Is Fleeting, Watches Are Forever

In luxury watchmaking, craftsmanship is what establishes a watch brand as a legitimate luxury producer, as the watch must connect to a sense of heritage in the trade skill of actually building a watch. This in turn increases the quality perception of the watch and thus the value. In other words, a luxury watch must be something more than just another industrial product, otherwise it is simply not legitimate in the luxury world.

Therefore, the relationship between the luxury watch craftsman and the customer depends on the notion of authenticity, which is really just another way of describing legitimacy. It is no accident that the most prestigious luxury watch brands have their headquarters in remote areas located around the French/Swiss border, specifically in Jura, an area considered home to what we now know as the watchmaking tradition. By associating themselves with this region, brands establish themselves as authentic, since this association connects them to the timeless heritage and tradition of the world’s finest watchmakers.

### Why do people buy luxury watches?

Through this analysis, we know that the primary motivating factors of luxury watch consumption are functionality, aesthetics, and social status.

Functionality is perhaps the most obvious factor, as a watch is expected to accurately display the time. Not as clear, however, is the expectation of *how* the watch displays time, and via what mechanical functions and performances. In this category, the seller tends to focus on such things as battery life, durability, comfort, and pragmatism. Functional luxury watches might display the time, but they also might display the day, or have built-in world clocks.

Aesthetics then is the more intangible and hedonic elements of a customer’s motivation. In these instances, the watch itself is oftentimes considered as a piece of jewelry, with appearance and brand being more important than the actual function of the watch. These types of watches can be used as accessories, to complement a specific outfit, and can oftentimes be companion watches.

> The Apple Watch may keep perfect time, but it will not be timeless.

Luxury is typically defined by exclusivity, which is why major luxury brands advertise in major sporting events, and to people who could never afford their products. The goal of these campaigns is not to generate sales, but rather to elevate the brand above and beyond the reach of the ordinary consumer, not just in the buyer’s eyes, but in everyone’s eyes. This type of practice strikes at the core of socially driven motivation for people to become luxury customers, as the luxury industry is unique in the sense that the needs of customers are often driven by a willingness to fulfill a symbolic functional requirement, rather than obtain a useful functional utility.

As another example, when a customer buys a [Rolex submariner](https://www.rolex.com/watches/submariner.html), the purchased object is not only a watch, it is a device that can withstand full immersion in water up to depths of 300 meters. Most people purchasing this watch will never take it under the depths of the ocean, however, they have the ability to do so. This is a symbolic functional requirement as it is entirely unnecessary, yet the function itself is a key part of the motivation for the customer to purchase it in the first place. This type of consumption impulse, therefore, falls into the motivation of social status, rather than functional. This type of luxury consumption satisfies a customer’s psychological desire to become the desired self, as having a symbolic function creates a personal image and style that allows the owner to come close to what they want to be. In the instance of the Rolex Submariner, the owner likes the idea of being able to dive, and perhaps of actually engaging in the activity, even if they never actually will.

It is because of motivations such as this that certainly not all luxury watch consumers can be considered as fine connoisseurs enjoying the horological product for what it concretely is, namely a technical performance tool which has been developed through time and history. There is a significant part of the luxury watch clientele that is buying watches for their social meaning, making this category an authentic luxury sector where conspicuous motivation is very present.

Customers purchasing watches for functional reasons are principally seduced by the mechanical aspect and the product’s complexity, and can be described as connoisseurs who are interested in a brand especially for its contribution to the common watchmaking history, and not only for its marketing and advertising potential. Customers attracted by the design and the appearance of the watch tend to be those who wear a watch but still check the time on their phones, and therefore they wear it only for the beauty of the object.

### The Potential For Digital Disruption of Luxury Watches

For many luxury watchmakers, smartwatches may indeed threaten to disrupt the industry primarily due to the technology industry’s strong innovation potential. Traditional watchmaking places a strong emphasis on brand roots, traditions, and heritage, and even the younger new entrants in the luxury watch business must subscribe to this class heritage of watchmaking, or risk being deemed illegitimate.

> Whereas all Rolex watches are expensive, and exclusive, the Apple Watch has various versions that sell to vastly different customer profiles.

On the other hand, Apple has a history of creating market demand where there previously was none. Apple Founder and former CEO Steve Jobs was famously quoted as saying that he didn’t trust market research because he believed that consumers didn’t actually know what they wanted until they saw it. The luxury watch industry must conform to established norms and values of the luxury markets, whereas Apple is free to attack the status quo and time-honored traditions that have kept certain brands exclusive. Apple has already attempted to enter the luxury market via its partnership with [Hermes](https://www.hermes.com/fr/fr/), and several traditional watchmakers have tried to keep up by creating their own smartwatches.

At first glance, the smartwatch does not seem an immediate threat, as [according to a survey conducted around the time of the Apple Watch launch in 2015](https://blueshiftideas.com/reports/041503AppleWatchWillDisruptMidtierTraditionalWatchmakers.pdf), 90% of traditional luxury watch owners stated that they were not immediately interested in the purchase of the Apple Watch. However, two-thirds of all respondents in the same study did mention that they will consider the Apple Watch when making future purchasing decisions. At the very least, this should behoove the luxury watch industry to consider the Apple Watch as a potential future competitor, and to better understand the motivation for purchasing it in the first place.

### It’s Not a Rolex

Out of the three primary motivating factors for purchasing luxury watches, smartwatches definitely fall into the functional category. While they are, indeed, successful tools that may be specialized in a number of functions, namely connectivity, they cannot appeal to the consumer motivations of aesthetics and social status. If Apple is going to compete in the luxury consumer market, they will need to penetrate the younger market, since the older demographics tend to be far more rigid and inflexible to innovation when it comes to luxury purchases.

> Since we know that luxury authenticity is rooted in craftsmanship, we can safely say that for this reason, the Apple Watch will struggle to position itself as a true luxury watch.

Apple and the smartwatch do have one primary weakness, and that is obsolescence. Technology products are notorious for not lasting very long, and once they break, they are essentially worthless, whereas traditional watches can function for decades, or longer, and if they break, there is a strong likelihood that they can be repaired. Ephemerality can, therefore, indeed be considered as a major weakness of the smartwatch when competing with credible luxury products. The Apple Watch may keep perfect time, but it will not be timeless.

The most expensive Apple Watch to date was made with rose gold and sapphire crystal, and had a price tag of around $20,000 USD. This one version of the Apple Watch is indeed in the same price range as major traditional luxury watch producers. However, unlike the traditional luxury competitors, the Apple Watch will become technologically obsolete in a few years, whereas the traditional luxury watches will be timeless, and may actually increase in value as time passes.

Unlike most luxury brands, the Apple Watch is not positioned exclusively for the luxury category. Whereas all Rolex watches are expensive and exclusive, the Apple Watch has various versions that sell to vastly different customer profiles. The rose gold Apple Watch for instance may sell to a wealthy individual or a celebrity, while the most affordable version of the watch might be adorned by a high school student with part-time employment. This certainly does not help Apple position itself as a luxury brand.

### Are luxury and tech compatible devices?

Both smartwatches and traditional watches are inspired by the need for control, and the human instinct to master time. Smartwatches expand upon this concept and provide users with added functionality for achieving this. Examples of this are the agenda, reminders, notifications, and personal monitoring. The Apple Watch gives its owner the ability to be far more efficient and available while enhancing their ability to measure their social, physical, and professional performance. None of these functions, however, are enough to make the Apple Watch a legitimate luxury product. Since we know that luxury authenticity is rooted in craftsmanship, we can safely say that for this reason, the Apple Watch will struggle to position itself as a true luxury watch.

> A Rolex will almost certainly be passed from grandfather to grandson, whereas a single customer will see several generations of Apple Watches processed through recycling centers in just a couple of decades.

Another problem for the Apple Watch is that several market studies have shown that luxury customers tend to develop a very special emotional relationship to the traditional luxury watch and that the watch itself arouses in the customer a sense of attachment and fidelity. Many luxury watch customers also have a near ethereal attachment to the watch, which oftentimes imparts a spiritual quality to the object itself. This customer perception that the watch is in a sense alive, invokes a profound sense of timelessness and authenticity. Because of the electronic and obsolescence factors, Apple cannot recreate this experience with a digital watch.

In the instance of a traditional luxury watch, an artist designs and builds something following centuries-old practices that have consistently delivered the utmost quality and value. The smartwatch, as it is today, is at least in part mass-produced in a factory that utilizes many different cost savings techniques. Furthermore, standardized software must be loaded onto the watch, and the device must be charged with electricity in order to provide any functionality.

Simply put, traditional watches are very good at creating emotional attachment, while smartwatches struggle to do so, primarily because there is no sense of heritage, craftsmanship, or timelessness.

Rarity is also very important to the luxury industry as a whole, and this is especially true for jewelry and watches. This concept of rarity comes from a perception that something is difficult to find and craft, and luxury watches accomplish this further by using limited, hand-selected, and therefore prestigious raw materials. Add to this formula intense craftsmanship that requires a tremendous amount of skill, and you have an exclusive luxury watch.

Smartwatches, on the other hand, don’t follow this rarity principle that applies to luxury, simply because they cannot. In order to maintain and manage a cost-efficient supply chain, companies like Apple must use economies of scale, and therefore it is impossible for them to use small quantities of limited materials in their manufacturing. Indeed, as high-tech objects, the Apple Watch is produced on a mass-scale and is not the result of any kind of particular craftsmanship. For this reason, it is impossible for the smartwatch to generate an emotional connection based on the production method.

In order to be legitimate, the luxury watch must be perceived as timeless, and therefore, must be durable enough to last for a very long time, but also must have a design and appearance that are equally as timeless. Customers and brands oftentimes use the term *classic* to describe this principle. Smartwatches are in total contradiction of this because they will be functionally obsolete in only a few years, and further cannot escape the need for a digital display, which will never be timeless.

> The luxury watch category is to watches what vinyl is to music.

Timelessness can only be built at the very moment when the technology is mature, and as previously mentioned, the element of transmission and durability between generations is also very important for luxury watch consumers. A Rolex will almost certainly be passed from grandfather to grandson, whereas a single customer will see several generations of Apple Watches processed through recycling centers in just a couple of decades. Because of this principle, luxury watches oftentimes increase in value as time goes on, whereas Apple Watches become worthless within a single decade.

### Implications to Luxury Marketing

The smartwatch, in particular the Apple Watch, is to the watch industry what iTunes and Spotify were to the music industry. These innovations, while not brand new inventions, completely changed the market and consumption habits of music listeners. However, the traditional routes of music distribution prior to these online platforms were largely functional. In the luxury watch industry, history, heritage, and tradition are very important in order to attain legitimacy, and therefore, the disruption will not be felt as profoundly in those markets.

In a crude sense, this is similar to how some music enthusiasts still covet vinyl records and hold them to be more authentic and valuable than all other forms of musical recordings. The luxury watch category is to watches what vinyl is to music.

Furthermore, a luxury watch can last many generations, while functional watches will break or become obsolete, and lose value in the short-term. Luxury watches are investments, whereas all other watches are functional. It is therefore unlikely that the smartwatch can outright replace the luxury watch since it simply does not match with the values of the luxury watch customer, in this regard.

As a product, the luxury watch is probably safe, however, the consuming habits of customers are always evolving and changing, and experience and personalization are becoming increasingly valued. In this respect, the Apple Watch could be a convincing luxury product if Apple and its partners are able to position the watch as a gateway to a luxury experience, rather than as a traditional luxury object, subject to the codes of the luxury watch industry. The question then becomes, will the Apple Watch, and other smartwatches, be able to convert customers from object driven luxury to experience-based luxury?

Historical quotes, supposedly from wise and amazing people, have become like drugs for the disillusioned and broken. You hear these seductive little strings of word porn all the time by people who are trying to convince you of something, and so they invoke the faux wisdom of someone else who was supposedly a lot smarter than both of you.

There are, indeed, many real and excellent quotes out there, by truly brilliant minds. Unfortunately, when it comes to self-help, motivation, and productivity coaches, those quotes get entirely ignored, because they require critical thinking.

Instead, the false pushers of blind-faith-inspiration and spiritual-self-aggrandizing tend to spew bite-sized quotes by people who are popularly considered to have been wise, or otherwise extremely important and successful.

The problem is that these quotes are always either taken out of context, attributed to the wrong people, or are entirely fabricated. The result is that someone thinks they are receiving wisdom from someone credible and noble, when in fact, it could be the words of a murderous rapist who was obsessed with penis art.

Below are a few popular examples:

## Albert Einstein

> “The definition of insanity is doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results.” — Not Einstein

It’s important to note that this is not the definition of insanity — it’s also important to mention that Albert Einstein never said anything like this, ever.

This famous quote was actually coined in the [early 1980s by either Alcoholics Anonymous or Narcotics Anonymous](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2017/03/23/same/). Indeed, it has a nice ring when you say it to a drug addict, but it makes no sense in any other context.

According to the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/insanity), the actual definition is this:

> ***Insanity:***

> ***1)*** *The condition of being seriously mentally ill.*

> ***2)*** *An action that is stupid and likely to have extremely bad results.*

For those dealing with addiction, the second definition makes sense since their repeated actions of using and/or drinking are stupid, and will likely result in an extremely negative outcome.

However, in all other contexts, repeating the same thing over and over again is actually the path to improvement — we call this practice. In many eastern religions, this is also the path to enlightenment — they call this meditation.

Practice and mediation are good. Examples of things that require practice include learning how to play a musical instrument, training for sports, learning math, and learning a new language. Examples of the benefits of meditation include lowered stress levels, better sleep, and reduced anxiety.

In other words, this misattributed nugget of wisdom was meant for the most desperate of people who were struggling with extreme addition — it was perhaps attributed to Einstein [because his good friend and intellectual sparring partner, Niels Bohr, said something similar, albeit with an entirely different meaning:](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/einstein-s-parable-of-quantum-insanity/)

> “Naïveté is doing the same thing over and over, and always expecting the same result.” — Niels Bohr

Again, Bohr said basically the opposite of the popular self-help insanity quote, since Bohr knew that the universe was oftentimes unpredictable and chaotic. In regards to self-help, the quote misattributed to Einstein is entirely meaningless.

## Archilochus

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*cZWC5HIVHORgGqaM)

Photo by [Alexander Abero](https://unsplash.com/@alexabero?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

Over the past three years, the world has seen a series of major supply chain disruptions caused by the pandemic, political tensions, climate change, natural disasters, and even shipping accidents. As a result, firms are increasingly keeping an eye on their balance sheets, since the next supply chain disruption might very well be the one that brings them down.

Supply Chain Finance (SCF) has, therefore, become a highly important and trending topic since it helps firms throughout the value chain attain funds earlier, thus they may have quicker access to working capital for financing future operations.

SCF is also the [best-known business practice for eliminating waste and excess since it shines a bright light on the total costs of both](https://www.paymentsjournal.com/supply-chain-finance-the-next-wave-of-business-growth/). If more firms implemented SCF methods in their business planning and operations, it would be good for both their balance sheet and for the planet.

In other words, SCF forces businesses to see the bigger picture and understand the full ecosystem that they exist within. Therefore, SCF causes businesses to make decisions based upon the sustainability of not only themselves but of every firm and customer that they depend upon. The result is that businesses begin to work with each other as partners, and are incentivized to help each other, rather than employing a ruthless set of competitive tactics aimed at improving the bottom line.

### Why are supply chain disruptions so destructive?

Anytime a manufacturer produces something, they must first purchase parts and materials from other firms, which are the suppliers. If one or more of those suppliers is delayed in delivering on time, then the entire chain gets held up, all the way down to the customer.

While this poses its own set of problems for the products directly affected, the real threat to many businesses is that this situation makes it impossible to produce other goods and services as well.

Why is that?

When a delay in the supply chain occurs, the money for facilitating that exchange is tied up with the delayed suppliers. In other words, if a manufacturer has already paid for components from a supplier, but there was a delay during delivery, then the manufacturer has to wait for that shipment to arrive before they can finish production, and subsequently sell their goods to generate more cash, which they then use to purchase components for other products.

Simply put, a supply chain disruption on one product can easily affect all products.

While there are a number of ways to take on debt for financing during these instances, all of these options come with a cost. For smaller firms, those costs can threaten the financial health of the business.

Remember when the [massive container ship from China, *Ever Given*, was wedged into the shoreline of the Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Suez_Canal_obstruction), blocking it for six days?

The result was that hundreds of shipping vessels had to wait in line for more than a week. The alternative was to sail around the entire African Continent. During that episode, if you were a manufacturer with your cash tied up in components aboard one of those ships, then you likely lost a lot of money, or worse.

So how does Supply Chain Finance help prevent and resolve problems within the supply chain?

Simply put, by leveraging IT systems and business processes that facilitate quicker payment cycles, improved logistics planning, enhanced business relationships and contracts, favorable freight terms, and strong compliance programs, firms can greatly improve both the predictability and speed of their cash flow.

The result is enhanced working capital planning and financial management, which allows for actionable contingency plans during times of crisis and disruption.

Supply Chain Finance methods can be used to manage working capital during all the various growth phases of a firm, and to examine the many challenges and risks to firm sustainability. By properly understanding the financial impact of supplier and distributor relationships, inventory planning and purchasing, freight terms, business contracts, and payment cycles, firms of all shapes and sizes can become better partners for their suppliers, distributors, and customers.

### **So, what does Supply Chain Finance do exactly?**

The simplest way to explain it is that SCF looks for all the inefficiencies in a firm’s operations that cause the business to have less money to invest in its activities. The goal of SCF is to create strategic financial and operating plans that reduce financing costs for both buyers and sellers, enhance strategic partnerships throughout the value chain, and improve service levels to customers.

In other words, suppliers gain quicker access to cash to finance operations, buyers get more time to pay off their balances and reduce their costs, and distributors gain improved lines of credit. The result is happier customers, reduced waste, and healthier balance sheets at all nodes of the supply chain.

The key difference between an SCF approach and a traditional sales-focused market share approach is that SCF seeks to optimize working capital on the balance sheet, rather than maximize the number on the bottom line.

**Some examples of key business aspects that SCF seeks to analyze and improve are:**

- Supplier, distributor, and B2B customer contracts & agreements, for enhancing business relationships and partnerships.
- Selling customer and distributor invoices to financial firms to improve cash flow (invoice factoring).
- Reducing waste and eliminating excess and obsolete inventory.
- Bank loans vs. investor loans for improvements to logistics and inventory management.
- Costs, efficiency, and liability of freight and transportation terms.
- Demand Planning and operational compliance to improve working capital planning.
- Distribution contracts vs. operational compliance and inventory plans.
- Financing of key supplier operations via improved treasury management.
- Benefits and shortcomings of IT and Business Intelligence supply chain solutions (ERP, EDI, Data Management, BI, etc…).
- Supply chain fusion technology for automating receivables and payables management and improving cash flow.
- Streamlining cash flow, accelerating cash collection, and managing credits made available to distributors and B2B customers.

### **Powering the future**

Imagine that you run a medium-sized [LED light manufacturer](https://www.any-lamp.com/blog/led-reduced-co2-emissions-half-billion-tonnes) with a complex value chain that must be fully synchronized for you to stay competitive. The mission of your company is to [reduce energy consumption around the globe](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/led-lighting), and additionally, your product actually helps [reduce landfill waste and carbon emissions](https://www.any-lamp.com/blog/led-reduced-co2-emissions-half-billion-tonnes).

Several of your suppliers work closely with you to ensure your production floor gets the components and materials that you need on time, on budget, and at the quality that you demand. While you are technically the customer, your firm is just as dependent upon those suppliers as they are on you.

On the other side of the business are a number of distributors, all of various sizes and expertise. While one or two of your distributors depends upon your products to round out their product assortment, the rest have options for other suppliers of LED lights.

At the end of your value chain is your customer, who you depend upon to continue to purchase, use, and promote your product. However, the customer can only do this if they have access to the goods that you produce.

This is where the relationship with distributors becomes vital. Imagine for a moment that one of your distributors runs out of cash and cannot purchase enough inventory of your LED lights to meet the demand of your customers.

What do you do in that instance?

Distributors are typically strapped for cash since they work on thin margins, and are oftentimes sitting on excess inventory that they are waiting to return. In order to continue the flow of your business, you need to make sure that this distributor has enough working capital to procure your products.

Now, if you were smart and used SCF solutions to manage your supply chain, then you would be able to offer this key distributor payment terms where they don’t need to pay you within the normal cycle. It may sound simple, however, accomplishing this requires not only managing your own side of the street but also understanding well the operations and cash situations of both your suppliers and distributors.

Furthermore, many times issues like this exist not because of inefficiencies, but instead because of prioritization.

For example, if this distributor didn’t have the cash needed to pay quickly because it used it to purchase inventory from a different firm, then you could consider offering payment term incentives to encourage the distributor to pay you first before other suppliers.

Again, doing this comes at a cost, and understanding the cost vs. benefit of these options is what SCF does.

### Putting it all together

The major advantage of the Supply Chain Finance approach is that it is not focused exclusively on increasing margins on the P&L. If more firms implemented SCF methods, then they would be able to better partner with one another during times of crisis and disruption.

If businesses are incentivized to see the big picture and examine their ecosystem, then they will naturally adapt their strategy to a cooperative approach rather than an aggressively competitive one.

Supply Chain Finance might not be the only answer for sustainability in business, but it is certainly one of them.

### Tesla is not profitable

Tesla’s net income of $721 million in 2020 turns into a substantial loss if those regulatory credit sales are not considered.

What are regulatory credits?

In America, many U.S. states award regulatory credits to automakers for selling electric vehicles. Automakers must acquire a minimum number of these credits to comply with regulatory requirements. These credits can be bought and sold, so an automaker that doesn’t sell enough electric vehicles can buy credits from other automakers that do.

Tesla has a surplus of these credits relative to the minimum required to meet government emissions standards — this is because they are, in fact, an automotive company. Tesla has thus sold these surplus credits to other automakers, such as Toyota or General Motors, whose limited electric vehicle production means that they wouldn’t otherwise achieve compliance with regulatory standards.

In other words, Tesla became profitable by selling regulatory compliance to other automakers who produce gas guzzling vehicles.

Of course, this situation is not going to last forever, as other automakers are aggressively ramping up their electric vehicle efforts.

In other words, there is no reason to believe that Tesla has any real advantage in the EV market beyond that Elon Musk says so.

General Motors, for example, is [investing billions into its electric vehicle efforts](https://www.fool.com/investing/2020/11/19/general-motors-goes-even-bigger-on-electric-vehicl/?utm_source=nasdaq&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=article&referring_guid=1ca925f9-9f82-4d9d-b3d8-aef16ecbffeb), as the Detroit-based automaker has plans to launch dozens of new EV models within the next few years. GM also has [a new commercial electric vehicle brand](https://www.fool.com/investing/2021/01/12/general-motors-challenges-ford-and-workhorse-with/?utm_source=nasdaq&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=article&referring_guid=1ca925f9-9f82-4d9d-b3d8-aef16ecbffeb), which already has a contract with FedEx.

Unlike Tesla’s contract with Hertz, this contract between GM and FedEx is actually real.

### Putting it all together

What Elon Musk will pay in taxes for a $20.8 billion sale of his stock is just about $5 billion — this assumes that he is not offsetting it with losses on other investments such as SpaceX or the Boring Company.

In other words, by “abiding by the will of the people,” Musk will realize an enormous capital gain that would under almost all circumstances be impossible. When Musk joined Tesla, he paid about [49 cents per share](https://www.forbes.com/sites/quora/2014/12/29/how-much-equity-did-elon-musk-get-from-investing-in-teslas-series-a/?sh=178330212a9f) for his initial investment, which has since split 5 for 1, so his cost basis is actually just under 10 cents per share.

In other words, Musk will pocket between $15–$16 billion in raw profit, and that’s the worst case scenario. If he doesn’t do it now, then he will almost certainly never get another opportunity.

Again, all of this is possible for no reason other than that he has convinced enough people that Tesla is worth over $1 trillion. In reality, it probably doesn’t matter how well Tesla performs over the coming years, because the valuation is now so extreme that a positive result for investors would require that absolutely everything goes right, including that all of the competition is stifled, and that the regulatory situation remains exactly the same.

In other words, Tesla can do very well and the stock could still fall apart. Elon Musk knows this, and he wants to cash in before it’s too late. Essentially, Elon Musk has perhaps pulled off the greatest Wall Street heist of all time.

If Musk truly wanted to do something for the people, he could hand over his newly minted $15 billion to a variety of philanthropic organizations — I have a feeling he will instead try to send the money to Mars.

For those of you who love Tesla, you can be right about Tesla becoming a top automaker, but you will still lose by investing in the stock. Even if Tesla manages to escape its dependency on regulatory credits, and instead starts manufacturing millions of EV’s for a profit, the firm valuation right now is so absurd that it simply won’t matter.

In the end, even Elon Musk will have to come back to earth.

### Food & Beverage | Mixology

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*BiDsvYDvIeAwmiP-_9qKtQ.png)

Photo by [John Howland](http://johnhowlandphotography.com/)

At the height of my bartending career, I was in charge of an elite beverage program at an ultra-luxurious [Napa Valley](https://www.napavalley.com/) resort, that was once found hidden within a beautiful redwood forest, and frequently visited by some of the world’s most wealthy and famous people. I have many crazy stories from my time working there, such as going on an outrageous shopping spree with a large moving truck and a billionaire's credit card, hosting a private concert with some of the world’s most legendary songwriters, and the time when I witnessed the sale of the world’s [most expensive wine](https://www.decanter.com/wine-news/auction-napa-valley-smashes-record-18511/) at a staggering $800,000!

In 2020, [much of the resort burned to the ground](https://napavalleyregister.com/news/local/napa-county-looks-at-meadowood-resort-rebuild/article_d99de626-fd17-5a3a-897b-0c8bc53d23e6.html) during a California Wildfire that consumed tens of thousands of acres, and so my memories are now just relics of a once great establishment.

While many of the tales I earned there are definitely for the ages, by far my favourite story was when we hosted a private event for the top 40 executives of the notorious investment firm, [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/).

One of the attendees would actually later become Donald Trump’s chief economic advisor.

During that night, I was tasked with creating one incredible and highly unique mixed drink that would be tray passed to the thirsty and controversial members of the global financial elite.

### Behind the cocktail

It was just a few years after the infamous financial collapse of 2008, and the chiefs of Goldman Sachs had become infamous for using [government bailout money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubled_Asset_Relief_Program) to earn themselves hundreds of millions in profit. In short, because of the infusion of taxpayer money, Goldman Sachs was able to undercut its competitors and establish its dominance as the second largest investment bank in the world, which is a status they continue to enjoy today. At the time, many people were suffering terribly and losing their homes, meanwhile, Goldman Sachs experienced massive growth and increased profits.

In other words, the 40 people in the room that night all had recently made large fortunes as a result of the great recession.

Today, Goldman Sachs manages over $900 billion of assets around the world, and its officers are considered to be globally powerful and influential. For the record, Goldman Sachs did, in fact, pay back the government in full with a generous 23% interest rate. Regardless, the ethics of their actions remain a debated topic.

The event that I worked that evening was for the top brass of Goldman, which at the time included both Goldman CEO Lloyd Blankfein and COO Gary Cohn, both of whom actually hovered around my bar the entire night.

The event itself was held in an exclusive building known as “The Wine Reserve,” a place where only the extremely rich could store their most private selections of wine, which had been custom-made by some of the industry’s top winemakers. The focus of the event that evening was the release of one of those red wines, which was paired with a delectable dinner created by one of the world’s best chefs.

My cocktail was meant to be both the opening and closing act.

### The priceless cocktail

Known simply as “Damiana,” [Liqueur de Damiana](https://www.damiana.net/) is an ancient Incan recipe that uses an herb of the same name, which itself has origins in the spiritual mythologies of the Incans. This is why the glass bottle for Damiana is modelled after a naked Incan Goddess.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*YUTB7WEjR2RBzzlCTOBjow.png)

Courtesy [Damiana Liqueur](https://www.damiana.net/)

Damiana was actually used to create the very first Margarita cocktail at [Hussong’s Cantina](http://cantinahussongs.com/home.html) in 1941. Legend states that a man named Don Carlos created the drink for the daughter of a German Ambassador who liked to eat the salt straight out of the dish at the bar. Perhaps Don Carlos was afraid to scold the daughter of an ambassador, or perhaps he fancied her, but either way, he devised a plan to prevent her from continuing to eat all of the salt that was meant for tequila drinkers, and thus he created a special drink for her with salt along the rim of the glass.

[Damiana](https://www.damiana.net/about-damiana/) was once used to treat sexual problems, and today it is still widely considered to be an aphrodisiac. We cannot know for sure if Don Carlos used Damiana out of necessity, or if it was because he wanted to serve the ambassador’s daughter an aphrodisiac, but regardless, the Margarita was first created using this unique liqueur.

As a nod to Don Carlos, that night, I decided to create a super unique variation of the super popular Mexican cocktail, one that was a nod to the roots of the original, but that also elevated the experience to a billionaire level.

I named the drink “Papalo” because the key ingredient was a rather obscure herb called pápalo, which is grown mostly in Mexico and Bolivia.

It was actually my highly discerning nightmare of a boss who decided to make it the featured drink for the Goldman execs, as it was his new favourite. The recipe was complicated and time-consuming since it called for the careful handling of delicate leaves, along with a laborious multi-step process that included foaming both pineapple and fresh egg whites.

Despite that, my boss wanted the drink to be passed around on trays, rather than be made to order. In short, this required mass producing these drinks, which was no simple feat.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*rcwR9kzyHezXbIRyVGOVYA.jpeg)

Papalo looks similar to this, but with a leaf garnish — photo by [Vitchakorn Koonyosying](https://unsplash.com/photos/49zDZsYaug4?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/search/photos/pineapple-cocktail?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)

The first person to ever drink Papalo was my boss, and his response was so positive that I thought I was going to be promoted. I wasn’t, however, I was granted access to the private garden that housed the rare herbs and vegetables for the three-Michelin starred restaurant. This move by my boss created a bit of a power struggle between him and the chef, but I was able to procure the needed ingredients to successfully make the drink that evening.

Similar to the Classic Margarita, the key ingredients of Papalo were Tequila, Damiana and fresh squeezed key lime juice, however, the addition of caramelized pineapple, unique herbs, and fresh egg whites, is what made this drink so special.

The caramelized pineapplecame in the form of a purée, which was first frozen so that it would be cold and frothy when used, however, thawed just enough to be measured out perfectly.

The Tequila used was a Reposado, meaning that it was aged in oak barrels for around six months. This process mellows the otherwise vegetal liquor and imparts some interesting flavours and aromas, however, it doesn’t reduce the vegetal bite of the Tequila too much.

Additionally, aside from pápalo herb, I used [Lime Basil](https://homeguides.sfgate.com/grow-lime-basil-27325.html), which is a difficult-to-find and impossible to substitute ingredient that adds a slight tanginess.

The egg white was reverse dry shaken, which means that first the cocktail was shaken on ice without the egg, then the liquid was separated from the ice and shaken like crazy with the egg white. This is one reason why having cold purée was advantageous.

After everything was mixed, the drink was [fine strained](https://www.thespruceeats.com/how-to-strain-cocktails-759342), meaning that it was poured through a [passoire](https://www.thespruceeats.com/how-to-strain-cocktails-759342). This created a layered effect that allowed for the foam from the pineapple and egg to layer on top of the liquid and create a soft bed for a single leaf of pápalo.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*hwaWv4tZqrLMb54uC0E2JQ.jpeg)

Original recipe by author

The drink was very well received, and I was invited to make it again another time. I declined, however, because I didn’t receive a single dollar in tips the entire night, and instead learned that working for billionaires simple doesn’t pay.

I soon after quit bartending for good and instead enrolled in graduate school, but the memory of this cocktail lives on!

Due to the difficulty of getting all of these ingredients, this drink may never be created again, however, for those who want a taste of the high life, it’s certainly worth the attempt.

Despite what many want to believe, there is only one way for the western world to meet climate, energy, and security goals, and that’s nuclear power. Nuclear power plants provide an incredible amount of energy, and they do it without any carbon emissions or harmful particulates that contaminate the air that all of us breathe. Based on existing resources, nuclear power plants are sustainable for at least the [next three centuries](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-long-will-global-uranium-deposits-last/), however, new exploration, advancements in technology, and improvements in extraction methods are predicted to [at least double this estimate over the next couple of decades](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-long-will-global-uranium-deposits-last/), but only if the required economic investment is available.

Unfortunately, anti-nuclear activists around the world have spent years fomenting fear about atomic power, and many people have bought into it. As a result, lawmakers throughout the western world have been pressured to close nuclear plants that were previously producing clean, reliable, and safe energy.

While anti-nuclear advocates have insisted that renewables such as solar and wind can replace nuclear, the reality of rolling back the supply of nuclear power has instead been an increased reliance on carbon-emitting fossil fuels. This is incredibly stupid because the only way to stop climate change is to greatly reduce the consumption of fossil fuels. Additionally, the nations that control much of the remaining supply of oil and gas, are nations such as Russia and Saudi Arabia, who are far from being friendly to the western world.

Nuclear power is likely the only way for us to continue to meet our energy demands while at the same time eliminating carbon emissions that are causing climate change, greatly reducing other forms of air pollution that cause hundreds of thousands of deaths each year, and establishing energy independence for free democracies around the world.

Denying this fact will undoubtedly lead the world to disaster.

### The German problem

One great example of a spectacular clusterbomb of stupidity regarding nuclear energy policy can be seen in Germany, where policy leaders have entirely dismantled that country’s nuclear power grid.

After the 2011 [Fukushima disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_nuclear_disaster), where a Japanese nuclear power plant experienced multiple meltdowns as a result of a major earthquake and subsequent tsunami, German public opinion of nuclear power plummeted around the world. Despite the fact that most top energy experts warned that nuclear phaseouts would crunch the energy supply at a time when countries were shifting away from fossil fuels in order to meet climate mandates, and that a resulting overreliance on oil and gas would further compromise national security, the German government stubbornly moved forward with shutting down 14 of its 17 nuclear plants, with the final three slated to be turned off by the end of 2022.

Of course, this quickly proved to be a terrible idea since Germany eliminated one pretend threat and traded it for a very real one — Russia.

Today, Germany is stuck in a bad situation where it must support the Ukrainians and NATO sanctions against Russia on one hand, yet work with Russia for fuel imports on the other. Furthermore, Germany has not been able to live up to the promises of establishing an entirely renewable power grid, as a significant portion of its energy is still being generated by fossil fuels.

### About face

The war in Ukraine has been a game changer for nuclear, as it has led even the most fervent nuclear critics to face the reality that reducing domestic nuclear energy production will result in a reliance on Russian fossil fuels, which of course is no longer a possibility for many. In fact, the [European Union recently decided](https://www.wsj.com/articles/gas-and-nuclear-power-can-be-green-under-new-eu-plan-11657108882?mod=article_inline) to classify nuclear energy as “green,” which potentially opens up billions of euros in investment that were previously unavailable for advancing the technology of atom splitting.

EU member states are also beginning to act on their own. For example, Belgium’s Green Party recently made a similar adjustment to their belief system when they extended the life of their country’s remaining two reactors by a whopping ten years. Poland, which was on the opposite end of the spectrum and had heavily invested in coal, is building its first nuclear plant, while the neighbouring Czech Republic also plans to construct four new reactors over the next several years.

France, a country where 70% of its energy comes from nuclear, was also headed down the path of denuclearization and planned on shuttering 14 of its 58 reactors. That has quickly changed, however, as the French are now doubling down on nuclear energy, including next-generation designs. It would seem that France has no interest in winding up in a similar situation as their German neighbours.

Ironically, Germany may soon find itself needing to purchase nuclear-generated energy from France, as relations with Russia continue to sour.

To the north, the Dutch government is planning on building two new plants, and they have even gone so far as to plead with Germany to keep its remaining three nuclear plants online.

The USA, which has closed 12 reactors since 2012, is now allocating [$6 billion in aid](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-establishes-6-billion-program-preserve-americas-clean-nuclear-energy-infrastructure) to some of its struggling nuclear plants. Meanwhile, private investors and various government agencies are pouring billions of dollars into next-generation nuclear designs, while individual states are repealing long-standing bans on nuclear power. Wyoming recently convinced Bill Gates to build his [first fast reactor there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TerraPower), while NuScale, the company that created the first approved commercial small modular reactor design, is building several next-generation reactors in Idaho.

The story is similar in Asia and the Pacific.

Japan plans to reverse its denuclearization policy and is planning on bringing nine new nuclear reactors fully online this winter. Additionally, its 2030 carbon emissions goal is based on restarting a total of 30 dormant reactors.

South Korea recently announced its goal to boost nuclear generation by 30% over eight years, while Indonesia, Vietnam, and the Philippines are all investing heavily in new nuclear energy. Indonesia in particular has passed legislation to begin building its first nuclear power station.

Even China is in the nuclear game, as they have now established themselves as the world’s single largest investor in new nuclear energy projects.

### History repeats itself

Despite what is obvious to much of the rest of the world, Germany remains stubborn. Perhaps it is pride, or perhaps they are just that foolish — whatever the reason, the German government remains on pace to decommission all remaining plants by the end of this year.

German Economy and Climate Minister Robert Habeck [said that](https://abcnews.go.com/International/wireStory/correction-germany-nuclear-shutdown-story-82051054), “by massively increasing renewable energy and accelerating the expansion of the electricity grid we can show that this [non-nuclear energy] is possible in Germany,” while Environment Minister Steffi Lemke [declared that](https://abcnews.go.com/International/wireStory/correction-germany-nuclear-shutdown-story-82051054), “Nuclear power plants remain high-risk facilities that produce highly radioactive atomic waste.”

> “Never underestimate the power of stupid people in large groups” — George Carlin

The truth in Germany is that renewable energy sources delivered only 46% of the electricity generated in 2021, while fossil fuels accounted for over 41%. Meanwhile, the remaining three nuclear power reactors, the very same that Germany plans on closing later this year, provide over 13% of total power.

Given that there are no fast renewable options that can be implemented, the Germans will likely find themselves a majority fossil fuel country once again, one that depends upon a hostile Russia for oil.

It is indeed ironic that the man responsible for discovering how to harness the power of the atom was a German who fled his homeland because the government of his day would have persecuted him for being Jewish.

That man was Albert Einstein.

Today, the Germans are once again persecuting nuclear and driving out potential innovators for no good reason other than misguided fears of a fake threat, and once again, they will pay the price for placing baseless ideology over ethics, science, and common sense.

While the proud denizens of the Deutschlands might prefer an unfriendly alliance with war-mongering authoritarians versus admitting their folly over giving up on clean and safe nuclear energy, the rest of the world should take note of Berlin’s mega mistake, and get on board with the power of the atom.

### So how many people have actually died as a result of nuclear power?

Over the roughly 70 years that nuclear power has existed, there have been a total of 667 nuclear power plants that have ever operated, and only three major accidents have taken place. Using the [official internationally-recognized death statistics for those three disasters](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-world-energy-statistics-2020) that occurred at Three Mile Island, Chernobyl, and Fukushima, the combined loss of lives from the three major incidents is 32 people.

By this estimate, the number of deaths caused by the nuclear energy sector overall is the least of any energy sector.

For comparison, one hydroelectric dam failure killed 26,000 people in China in 1975, another killed 75 people in Russia in 2009, and in 2017, 180,000 people had to suddenly evacuate their homes in Northern California as a result of a damaged spillway that threatened to flood the surrounding area.

Regarding solar and wind, every year hundreds of people die as a result of electrocution, falling accidents, and other hazards that can occur with the construction and installation of solar panels and wind turbines.

Regardless, renewables and green energy are not the real culprits for deaths; that honour rests firmly with fossil fuel energy. The truth is that turning away from nuclear almost always means a heavier reliance on fossil fuels, which in turn claims the lives of hundreds of thousands of people every single year.

Climate change alone is believed to cause countless deaths annually, but many deaths also occur during mining, drilling and construction — this is to say nothing of the impact on respiratory and cardiovascular health. [By one estimate](https://www.engineering.com/story/whats-the-death-toll-of-nuclear-vs-other-energy-sources), the loss of life from fossil fuels is estimated at 100,000 deaths for every 1000TWh of energy supplied by burning coal, 36,000 deaths for oil, and 4000 deaths for natural gas.

To give some perspective, the world consumes 170,000TWh per year, and in 2020, oil supplied 32% of the world’s total energy, while coal supplied 27% and natural gas came in just shy of 23%.

### The Fukushima nuclear disaster was easily avoidable

What happened in Fukushima was the result of an [extremely rare 9.1 magnitude megathrust earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami) that triggered an incredibly powerful tsunami with waves as high as 45 meters (133 feet) that directly struck the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant that had been foolishly built along the earthquake-prone coastline of the Pacific Ocean.

The earthquake was the most powerful ever recorded in Japan’s history, and the fourth most powerful in the entire world since seismic recording began in 1900. Damage from the resulting enormous tsunami was actually even worse, with powerful surges utterly destroying entire towns as far inland as 10km (6.2 miles) from the coast.

Regardless of how extreme and rare the natural disaster that caused the nuclear disaster actually was, there were, indeed, many warnings prior to 2011 about the safety of the power plants in Fukushima.

In 1990, the U.S. [Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Regulatory_Commission) (NRC) declared that the failure of emergency electricity generators, and the subsequent loss of vital cooling systems for reactors, was of significant risk for nuclear plants located in seismically active regions; this was said to be one of the most likely risks for causing a meltdown. The Japanese [Nuclear and Industrial Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_and_Industrial_Safety_Agency) (NISA) actually cited this American report in 2004, and sent it to both the Japanese government and the power company that owned the power facilities in Fukushima — [no action was taken](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2011-03-16/japan-s-reactor-risk-foretold-20-years-ago-in-u-s-nuclear-agency-s-report).

Additionally, [Tokyo Electric Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Electric_Power_Company) (TEPCO), which operated the plants at Fukushima, was warned by engineers that their seawalls were insufficient to withstand a powerful tsunami — again, nothing was done.

Despite ignoring warnings and suffering a direct hit from one of the worst natural disasters of modern history, the nuclear disaster at Fukushima wasn’t nearly as bad as it could have been. In the end, only a single person died from radiation, and only eighteen more were injured severely — this is far from the fear-mongering claims of antinuclear activism that warn of entire cities being wiped out by future accidents at nuclear power plants.

More telling perhaps is the fact that there was another nuclear power plant, Fukushima Daini, just 12km (7.5 miles) south of the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant, which is where the disaster happened. At Fukushima Daini, no meltdowns, explosions, or radiation leaks occurred whatsoever. Following the earthquake and Tsunami, the four reactors at Fukushima Daini suffered extensive damage, yet the reactors [automatically shut down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scram) and the plant was back under control within two days.

What happened at Daiichi and Daini is actually a testament to how safe nuclear power plants actually are, even when they are built along the Pacific coastline just over tectonic plate fault lines that are capable of unleashing megathrust earthquakes of biblical proportions.

Rather than look to what happened in Fukushima as a reason to eliminate nuclear altogether, the better approach would be to construct nuclear plants in safer locations.

### The way through is forward not backwards

Existing nuclear energy relies on a process called fission, where a heavy chemical element is split to produce lighter ones. It is these types of reactions that cause heavy amounts of radiation that can poison, well, everything.

Nuclear fusion, on the other hand, is a process that works by combining two light elements to make a heavier one, and thus there is no significant radiation generated.

Scientists already know how to create fusion reactions; the problem is figuring out how to get more energy out of a fusion reaction than is required to start the process in the first place (aka the catalyst).

There is good news though.

Investment in nuclear technologies has allowed several labs around the world to discover new ways to harness nuclear fusion on Earth, in a way that would be sustainable, since the power generated from these experiments is rapidly nearing what is required to surpass the requirements for catalysts.

This is super exciting since fusion is the same process that powers the stars, including our own sun. The result of controlled fusion on Earth would be a virtually unlimited supply of low-carbon, low-radiation energy that produces almost zero hazardous waste.

Simply put, nuclear fusion is the game-changing technology that would move us from an advanced industrial society to one that more closely resembles [Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek).

Despite the incredible and very attainable promises of advanced nuclear technologies, fear of disaster has caused many to turn away and instead look to the incredibly unrealistic and unsustainable technologies of solar, wind, and geothermal, none of which generate nearly enough power to meet growing global demands.

These fears are unfounded and short-sighted, and they will kill millions of people if we don’t all get real very soon.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/2560/0*xfYqI7O3j5AwGqnW)

Photo by [Ibrahim Boran](https://unsplash.com/@ibrahimboran?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

In a perfect world there would be no need for money. If humans could work together in the John Lennon envisioned sort of way, then all things could be attained without having to account for any of it. Unfortunately, that’s not reality.

The [Tragedy of the Commons](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/tragedy-of-the-commons.asp) is a problem in economics that describes how there will always be some people who simply do not follow the plan. In other words, there will always be people who screw it up for everyone else.

Now, if we could easily identify who those people were, then it would be simple to round them up and send them off to some island somewhere, right?

> Wrong.

The situation in the Tragedy of the Commons largely exists because of a lack of information or trust, or both.

### Imagine All The People

Pretend that 10 people live by a pond that is plentiful with delicious and nutritious fish. Each day all ten people take exactly five fish for themselves. For a while this works okay, however, over time one of the people starts to notice that it seems as if there are not as many fish as before.

Now, rather than coordinate with the others, this one person instead decides to take 20 fish for himself and then not tell the others. Why did he do this? Because he was afraid that eventually the fish will run out, and he didn’t trust the others to provide for him if that ever happened. Rather than risk starving, he preemptively took more for himself.

This person might sound evil, but in reality, it could be anyone, even you.

While this example above is quite simple and extreme, many situations arise each day that challenge us to take less and leave more for others — most of the time we don’t do that.

**Answer the following questions honestly:**

- *Would you give up your car and instead take a crowded bus to work each day in order to reduce pollution?*
- *Are you willing to entirely stop eating beef to reduce the level of methane gasses in the atmosphere?*
- *Are you willing to stop traveling by airplane and instead take a train absolutely everywhere?*

If you answered **no** to any of these questions, then you are part of the problem in the Tragedy of the Commons — don’t worry I am to, so you’re in good company.

This is why we can’t just simply target certain people, because in reality, we all will do what’s best for ourselves under certain circumstances, because to not do so would be crazy.

In the example of the pond people, it would be crazy for our guy to expect the others to help him unless he knew them well — so what if he didn’t?

Would you trust a stranger to feed you?

**These are the key points to understand about Tragedy of the Commons:**

- The problem occurs when individuals neglect the broader well-being of society in the pursuit of personal gain — this includes self-preservation.
- This leads to over-consumption and ultimately depletion of the common resource, to everybody’s detriment.
- Tragedy of the Commons occurs when there is a scarcity of resources, which is true for almost all resources.
- The primary solutions to the Tragedy of the Commons are private property rights and government regulation.

What kind of government regulation?

Well, I’m glad you asked! There are several forms, but perhaps one of the best has been a type of accounting system that effectively tracks how much someone has contributed.

We call that system money.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*zSltZ8aXbR5mIAHV)

Photo by [Mathieu Stern](https://unsplash.com/@mathieustern?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

### Money Money Money

Money, as it is intended is essentially a ledger. If you have money, it’s because you received it for some service or labor that you provided to someone else.

Now… I know, the modern world is corrupt and vile, but just try to forget about all that for a moment.

**These are the key points to understand about Money:**

- Money is a medium of exchange; it allows people to obtain what they need to live.
- Bartering *was* one way that people exchanged goods for other goods before money was created.
- Just like gold and other precious metals, money has worth because for most people it represents something valuable.
- Fiat money is government-issued currency that is not backed by a physical commodity but rather by the stability of the issuing government.

Got all that? In other words, money today is guaranteed by nations, or a group of nations as is the case with the Euro.

But what is money based on? Most of you have probably heard of the Gold Standard, which meant that the U.S. dollar used to be based on the actual precious metal that we know as “Gold.”

However, for nearly a century now, money has not been based on gold.

What has it been based on?

Debt.

### Where Does Money Come From?

Yes. Debt. All money today, regardless of which currency or issuing government, is based upon the concept of debt.

If your head is spinning, don’t worry, it’s really not complicated.

Banks, you know, the kind that you go to when use the ATM, create money when they issue loans. For example, let’s say that you want a long-term loan to purchase a house, or as it’s commonly called, a mortgage. Each time that a bank issues one of these loans, they write on their balance sheet that someone owes them money — they call this an asset.

Now, it’s important to understand that the bank wasn’t simply sitting on a pile of cash that they handed over to you, instead, they just wrote in the total that you owe them, thus increasing their assets on their Balance Sheet.

Some of you who are more savvy may be saying “but what about the seller of the house, how do they get paid then?” Well, the bank sends them a check (so to speak) for the amount of the house.

Let’s say the final sale price of the house was $250,000. You as the buyer had to put down a down payment of $50,000, leaving the bank on the hook for $200,000. The bank sends a check to the seller for $250,000, and adds to their assets that you now owe the bank $200,000 — today. Each month that passes that amount increases, because of interest. By the time you finally pay back the bank, the total that they will receive from you is likely closer to $300,000.

This is because you gave them $50,000 up front, then they charge you interest on the $200,000 that they fronted to the seller.

So, in other words, the bank essentially created $100,000 in the economy via a loan to you for $200,000. Banks literally do millions upon millions of loans like this each year, many of which are in the hundreds of millions, or sometimes, the billions.

### What If They Run Out?

Another thing that happens quite often is that banks will loan each other money.

Why?

Well, think of it the mortgage example above and imagine that you go to your bank for the mortgage, however, at that exact moment that they don’t have $200,000 to pay the seller of the house that you want to purchase. Instead of telling you no, the bank will call up another bank and take a loan from them. The process is similar — the end result is more money in the economy.

Now, what if no banks have anything left to loan? Well, that’s where the Central Banks come in.

A bank can take a loan from a Central Bank, such as the Federal Reserve in the USA — the catch is that the cost of doing so is higher than if they go to a normal bank.

**These are the key points to understand about Money Supply:**

- Banks create money when they issue loans.
- Banks can borrow from Central Banks in order to meet reserve requirements.
- Loans from the Federal Reserve (Central Bank in the USA) are available via the *discount window —*a central bank facility that offers commercial banks very short-term loans.
- The rate charged to banks is known as the *discount rate*, which is usually higher than the rate that banks typically charge to each other — this encourages banks to only use the Central Bank as a last resort.

### Putting It All Together

Imagine that the world is simple. In that world, now imagine that you own a small farm and grow potatoes. Now, you want wine, because after digging up all those potatoes, you really need a big glass of tasty Chardonnay. The problem is that you don’t have Chardonnay.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*bq8UZ_mrD6AR-8oB)

Photo by [Rafael Barquero](https://unsplash.com/@rafael_barquero?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com?utm_source=medium&utm_medium=referral)

One of your neighbors grows sunflowers, and he wants to cook up a tasty recipe that uses sunflower seeds and potatoes. He offers to exchange some of his sunflower seeds for some of your potatoes, but you don’t need his seeds, you need Chardonnay. In the next town is a guy that has Chardonnay, but he doesn’t need potatoes or sunflower seeds, in fact, he doesn’t need anything (maybe that’s the effect of good Chardonnay.) In this scenario, exchange is impossible — this is where money comes in.

Instead of giving you seeds, your neighbor gives you money for your potatoes, which you then take and give to the guy with Chardonnay, who gladly accepts it because while he might not need anything today, next week he might find something that he can exchange that money for.

Voila, we have a market. This is why having money is important, it literally facilitates exchanges that would otherwise be impossible. It does this because it keeps track of who did something for someone else. At the end of the day, your neighbor is eating a yummy snack because he profited from your labor to grow and harvest the potatoes, while you have your glass of Chardonnay that the guy in the next town labored for months to make. Because he doesn’t need anything right now, and he has money, he is planning his next vacation.

In an ideal world, the people with the most money would be those who provided the most goods and services to others — this is because money is supposed to be reflective of human labor.

The reason that this exchange example above works is because the value of the money itself is guaranteed by a government who has authorized banks to offer loans to people who it deems worthy. This is how people are able to start up businesses, get education, build new homes, purchase transportation, and expand their operations.

So that’s it! You now understand what money is and where it comes from.

But okay, if it’s this simple, then why are so many people struggling to earn enough money?

There are a lot of reasons for this, one of the primary ones being the way the private ownership is applied. In our modern global high finance world, the people with the most money actually labor less than most others, at least in the traditional sense. The old ways of doing things required a lot of physical labor and attention — today, that’s not the case.

If you pose the question:

> ***“Where do people go to invest?”***

Most people would respond by saying,

> ***“The stock market.”***

While this is by far the most common response, it is, in fact, quite wrong.

All around the world, investing is carried out using a wide variety of financial instruments that are primarily traded on things called **exchanges.**

How many exchanges are there? A lot, but more on that later.

When people say “*The Stock Market*,” they are oftentimes incorrectly referring to the global financial system where people trade all types of things including money, metals, fruits, contracts, and pieces of companies.

Pieces of companies? Yes, and that is what people in the English-speaking world are talking about when they say **stocks.**

So where are stocks traded? On the Stock Market of course! And that is the only thing traded there. But what is that exactly?

## Taking Stock of Stocks

When someone buys a single stock, they are purchasing a small portion of a publicly traded corporation, aka, a company.

What does that mean “publicly traded?”

Well, simply put, it means that anyone with a little bit of money can purchase a portion of a company. If a company is private, then a person needs permission of the owners, regardless of how much money they have.

For example, if you have an extra $1,000, then you can easily go online and purchase a couple of shares in your favorite major corporations, as long as they are sold on a **stock exchange**. However, if you want to buy shares in the local café down the street, you can’t do it without permission from the owner, unless that café happens to be a Starbucks (SBUX).

Now, you have probably heard of the term **shares** being used to describe stocks — well that’s because that’s what stocks represent! Each single little stock represents a portion, or *share*, of a company.

So, when it comes to publicly traded companies, the ownership of those companies is quite literally being *shared* amongst everyone who possesses stocks, or shares, of that company.

Make sense?

If this sounds absurd, it’s probably because you, or someone you know, owned 10 shares of a major company such as Apple, Wal-Mart, General Electric, etc… and certainly, there were no invites to any owner meetings. Be that as it may, while perhaps there were no premium raw oysters aboard the corporate jet, owning 10 shares in any company means that, technically and legally, that a person holding shares is an *owner* of that company.

Now, if this proud owner of 10 shares was you, the reason why you didn’t get any special treatment is because there are literally hundreds of thousands, or perhaps even millions, of owners, and they won’t all fit on a private jet.

Major corporations issue millions or even billions of shares to the public, so having 10, 100, 1000, or even 10,000 shares is oftentimes not even a drop in a giant rain barrel!

As an example, Apple (APPL), the storied maker of the infamous iPhone, is one of the largest companies in the world. Currently, Apple has more than 16 billion shares outstanding!

### What’s The Point Of Buying Stocks Then?

So if we can’t get the corporate perks or have a say in how the company is managed, why buy stocks?

Well… because:

> ***Owning shares in a company is a way to own the future value of that company.***

In other words, if the company performs well is thus worth more than when you originally purchased it, then the portion of the company that you own increases in value! If one year ago you put $1000 towards purchasing 10 shares at $100 each, but today each share is worth $120, then the value of your share has increased by $200! If you decided to go and sell your shares to someone else, then you would profit by that amount.

Also, owners of stocks (this depends but let’s keep it simple for now) typically get to vote on important matters such as elections for the board of directors and officers of the company. It’s true, if you own only 10 shares your vote probably doesn’t count for much — but then the outcome of the vote probably doesn’t affect you as much either.

On the other hand, someone who owns 5% of all outstanding shares of a company has a lot more invested in the performance of that company than does someone who picked up 10 shares on the RobinHood App.

Of course, this is a bit subjective, but generally speaking, this is the idea.

## So Then What Is The Stock Market Exactly?

The term *stock market* actually refers to the entire collection of markets and exchanges around the world where the buying and selling of shares of companies take place.

There are many exchanges, too many to mention here… but don’t worry, let me make it simple:

The most famous stock exchange is the New York Stock Exchange (**NYSE**), or as it’s commonly affectionately called — **Wall Street**. While the NYSE is the oldest and most widely known of the exchanges, it is not actually the largest.

So which is the largest? The **Nasdaq**!

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*ImC-Ie5B4g7da30K.png)

Photo by Summer Sun on [Canva](https://www.canva.com/)

Unlike the NYSE, which has a physical address on Wall Street in New York City, the Nasdaq exists virtually and has no brick-and-mortar so to speak.

Now, while the management of Nasdaq is, indeed, based in NYC, Nasdaq is a global electronic marketplace, and so it doesn’t really *exist* anywhere.

Nasdaq was created in 1971 and was the world’s first electronic exchange, meaning that trading happened via computer rather than on pieces of paper inside a building somewhere — this is why most of the world’s tech giants, including Apple and Facebook, are listed on the Nasdaq.

The Nasdaq started out as a relatively obscure exchange, but as the tech industry flourished and became dominant, so too did the Nasdaq!

Another thing that the Nasdaq has that the NYSE does not is cryptocurrencies — more on that in another article.

**So here are the key takeaways:**

- The NYSE is old and stuffy and is primarily for large established firms.
- The Nasdaq is for fresh and techy companies and far more global in nature.
- The other major exchanges around the world include the FTSE in London, the Euronext in Europe, and the TYO (aka the NIKKEI) in, you guessed it, Tokyo.

Again, it’s important to remember that these exchanges have historically been for stocks only — The Nasdaq had broken rank by allowing cryptocurrencies.

In other words, stock exchanges do not trade currencies, commodities, or other types of financial **securities.** They do, however, allow for the trading of certain derivatives — more on that in future articles.

So when we say “Wall Street,” we mean the NYSE?

Yes, but no, but sort of yes. *Wall Street* has come to refer to the overall financial industry that manages investments, but at the center of it is the NYSE, which is where it all started — more on this in future articles as well.

## Putting It All Together

A stock exchange is where shares of companies are traded. Without stock exchanges, companies would not be able to offer shares of ownership to the public. Why is this important?

The reason why companies want to go public is because it can be an excellent way of raising money so that they can expand their operations — it can also be really great way to make the owners really super-wealthy, and quickly. However, going public can also be a great way to lose a lot of money fast and ultimately have to close your doors.

- Stock exchanges are themselves privately run organizations that are heavily regulated by governments.
- Just like any private organization, they flourish, merge, disband, or fail.
- The primary function of a stock exchange is to provide a marketplace for the trading company ownership.

When people who know what they are talking about say “The Stock Market,” they are referring to the overall collection of all the exchanges around the world that facilitate the trading of company shares.

Got it?

So what are dividends? How are exchanges regulated? How about derivatives? And what about all of the other things that are traded, such as currencies and commodities?

Well, all that will be covered in other articles!

Right now I am partially employed, but for several months I had absolutely nothing. Very recently, I was offered a full-time financial analysis management job in Florida, one which I would have eagerly accepted under normal circumstances. However…

> What? How? Why? Are you Crazy?

This is what I imagined hearing from every single one of my American friends and family members, and what a big part of me was saying to myself. I’m an American from Detroit. The culture I grew up in is one where work is honorable — not to work is downright shameful.

I wanted to take the job, and at first I told myself that perhaps it was time to move back to the good old USA.

But the bottom line was that I couldn’t accept it, because I simply couldn’t afford to take the job.

*What?*

Yes, that’s right. After a quick and sober and rational few minutes of reflection, I realized that there was absolutely no way moving back could make sense — it was quite literally jumping from the simmering frying pan into a giant gas-powered grill.

The job I was offered would have paid roughly $85,000 per year, offered three weeks paid vacation, medical coverage through Aetna or United, and a series of other perks including major discounts on company products and the possibility of annual bonuses.

Regardless, I said no, because I am better off unemployed in France than I am being fully employed in the USA.

Allow me to explain.

### Quick Backstory

After more than 30 years in the USA, I left three years ago to live in France. For those unfamiliar with the book *Dune* by Frank Herbert, “Melange” is a fictional naturally occurring drug that exists on the planet Arrakis. It is highly sought after because it gives the user a longer life span, greater vitality, and heightened awareness. Melange, aka “spice,” also makes it possible and safe to travel between solar systems. However, spice is also highly addictive, and withdrawal from it means certain death. Those who live on the planet of Arrakis long enough eventually become addicted just from breathing the air — as a result, their eyes become a deep shade of blue.

When it comes to living outside of the USA, color my eyes blue.

Notice I didn’t say living in France — no, I’m not addicted to France, I’m financially and culturally addicted to not living in the USA.

Before I left the USA, I was working for a corporation and earning what should have been a lot of money. In reality, I never really had any significant amount of money left at the end of the year.

I was frequently fainting because of stress and anxiety, and one time I almost passed out while driving my car.

I had problems with my teeth, and I spent thousands of dollars just trying to keep up with the most important and urgent work — this was *with* dental coverage.

In order to get to work, I needed to have a car, and that car cost me on average around $500 per month — this was with *no car payment* as I owned it outright.

Just before I had the opportunity to move to France, a nearby would-be-mass shooter with a stockpile of arms was busted by the police and found to be in possession of aerial maps of several nearby schools. Worse yet, the only reason he was caught was because he also had a stockpile of kiddy porn and the feds had discovered the porno ring that he was a part of.

I have a child, and each time that I would put her on the school bus I prayed to god that some lunatic wouldn’t decide to shoot up her school.

In hindsight, the fear for my daughters safety and future is probably what pushed me over the edge to take a huge risk and go for the Trans-Atlantic move. Beyond just the threat of violence, I also had to prepare lunch for her every day because the food in the cafeteria was so hideous that not even the teachers who worked there would subject their own children to it — I was worried about my child’s long term health and nutrition.

### The Move

Moving across the ocean as a single parent with a six year old wasn’t easy, and adjusting to the very different lifestyle and system in France was at times downright terrifying.

It never caused me to lose consciousness though.

Even if it had, the medical system here would have taken care of me and at a price that I would have been able to afford. In the USA, I had paid over $4,000 out of pocket to try and figure out why I was passing out. Here, I paid €35 to find out why I wasn’t.

Just days before getting on the flight I had a tremendous pain in my jaw. I took an appointment with a dentist in America who told me that it would cost $2800 to fix it — again, *with* insurance. When I expressed my concern about the cost, his reply was “so money is kind of tight right now?”

No. I was fine, it’s just that $2800 is a lot of fucking money to drop last minute, especially just to stop the pain — this is the attitude in America, if you can’t afford it, it’s not the system, it’s you.

It took quite a while to sort everything out, but when I finally got to a dentist, I paid just over €300 out of pocket to fix my problem.

In the USA, I sold my car for $5,000 and I used the money to help finance my move. In France, I pay €70 a month for all of my transportation and I don’t need a car.

Enrolling my daughter in school was rather straightforward. Sure, there were issues, but at the end of the day she was in a good school that was well protected and quickly taught her how to speak French. In addition, they actually fed her nutritious food.

### **Back To The Job Offer**

In France I am only partially employed, but I am actually able to manage the situation and have a furnished apartment in a good neighborhood, a cell phone, a computer, internet, TV, good food, wine, proper education for my daughter, and very importantly, access to medical, dental and vision.

If I had taken the job in America at $85k per year, I would have had to spend at least 70% of my net income to afford what I have here, and I would have had less vacation time. This is true because in the USA, regardless of where you are, it costs on average 5x more to have a cell phone, internet, and the most basic transportation. When it comes to food there is simply no comparison — to eat well in the USA costs a lot while in France it’s considered a basic need.

The biggest risk, however, was that if I had moved to Florida, my employer could have subsequently fired me at any time, for any reason. If that would have happened, I would be completely fucked and would lose everything.

Here in France, if you have a job, you are guaranteed the income unless you commit some type of egregious offense. In the event of a layoff, the social security system provides a similar income for up to two years. If you do not have a job, you will not die, and will still have basic protections and coverage.

Bottom line, I just couldn’t afford to work in America.

This is how bad things have become in the USA — my daughter and I are safer, more comfortable, better fed, and better prepared for the future while only partially employed in France than fully employed in the USA.

Going from barely employed in France to fully employed in the USA just simply didn’t make financial sense.

Pretend for a moment that you have a lot of money — let’s say $1 million (or € or £ or what have you.)

There are quite a few things you could do with that $1 million, such as buy a big boat, a really nice house, throw lavish parties, or start a dog spa for dog lovers!

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*9iN4yvGY7m50riLM.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

But let’s assume for a moment that you are afraid of the world, and so, you want to be ready for anything. Rather than put your money to work, you stuff that money safely inside a mattress and keep it hidden. 20 years later, you are feeling better about the world, and so you decide to pull that money out of the mattress and use it to purchase a house in an awesome neighborhood.

Unfortunately, you are quickly disappointed to discover that the houses you can purchase are all in wretched neighborhoods, the boats are all used and dingy, the parties are lame, and it’s not even possible to purchase all the needed luxury items for that dog spa.

**Why did this happen? Well… you shouldn’t have slept on all that money.**

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*0FgxvwvOcC2ytOwX.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

Now, you already know what happened — **inflation**! But wait, what exactly *is* inflation?

## Prices Go Up Up And Away!

**Inflation** is a widely used term that people rather casually throw around whenever they talk about how money isn’t worth the same today as it was in the good old days.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*tyNp-uj7blRqx-pj.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

While it’s certainly interesting to hear your grandpa talk about how an entire bucket of fried chicken used to be $1, it isn’t enough to help you make sound investing decisions and plan for your retirement.

Sorry Grandpa.

In order to understand ***why*** inflation happens, and thus why the entire world subscribes to the all-important philosophy of **time value of money**, we need to understand, philosophically, why everyone agrees with each other that the value of something changes over time.

> ***“Time crumbles things; everything grows old under the power of Time and is forgotten through the lapse of Time.”***

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*83H5AegxRAlYg91-.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

As Aristotle, correctly explained, time ultimately ruins everything and reduces it to nothing more than dust in the wind — money is no different.

Maybe you’re asking, “how could that be? Money is created by people, so can’t we just control its value?

Well actually, yes! However, history has shown us that if we do that too much, then we tend to screw things up quite a lot. To avoid making these types of mistakes, modern governments try to avoid manipulating their currencies too much.

What’s important is this:

> ***It’s not actually time itself that causes money to decrease in value — rather, it’s transactions that take place over time.***

## The Costs Of Doing Business

I’m sure you’ve all read books by John R. Commons and Ronald Coase on the concept and construction of **Transactions Costs** and the economic impact on global monetary policy — I mean, who hasn’t? Right?

In all seriousness though, if you haven’t…

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*y2f63xsmQyj4n0aL.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

Now, I’ve said many times that Finance is not complicated, and that’s because it’s not. Transaction costs, on the other hand, are very, very complicated. It’s okay though because Transactions Costs aren’t really a Financial thing, they are an Economic thing.

What you need to know is this:

> ***Every time exchange happens there is a cost, and this cost causes prices to go up. The more transactions that occur, the more prices increase.***

Make sense? No? It’s okay, stay with me. Here’s an example:

## Two Worlds, Two Beers, Two Prices

Imagine there are two worlds that are exactly the same, but the people living in them do not know about each other. In both worlds, they drink beer, and lots of it… so someone has to make it!

Now, even if you don’t know how beer is made, you probably have a basic idea of what the end result should be.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*51F2zQOc946I5tj9.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

**In the first World, we’ll call it “Alpha,” we have the following:**

1. Farmer *Bob*, who grows the barley and the hops.
2. *Fred*, who owns the dying racks and processing equipment for making the barley and hops ready for brewing.
3. *Stan*, the guy who owns all the brewing equipment.
4. *Sheila*, who owns the trucks to get the beer to the customers.

**Alpha world looks like this:**

Each one of these points is what we call a **node** along the **supply chain** for producing beer and delivering it to the customers who enjoy drinking it. As you can see above, there are four nodes before we get to our final product: Beer.

Each step of the way in the above beermaking process, there are transactions. In each one of these transactions, there are costs.

Why?

Because each person who contributes to the overall supply chain needs to profit.

Got that?

Farmer Bob has to gain a surplus for selling his barley & hops to Fred, who then dries it and processes to make it ready for brewing; Fred then sells it to Stan, who then turns it into beer, which is then sold to Shiela to drive to the lucky people chugging it down!

Since each of these people needs to be rewarded for their labor, the price of the product goes up at each node.

**Here’s how it works:**

1. Bob sells the barley and hops to Stan for **$1.00** a bushel (whatever the heck that is).
2. Fred processes what be bought from Bob, then sells it to Stan for **$2.50**.
3. Stan turns what he got from Fred into Beer, then sells it to Shiela for **$5.00** (hey, beermaking ain’t easy.)
4. Sheila then delivers the beer, but since she needs to cover her costs plus earn a profit, she sells each beer for **$6.50.**

**Now, let’s go to the second world, we’ll call this World “Beta.”**

1. Farmer *Bob* grows AND processes the Barley & Hops.
2. *Stan* brews the beer.
3. *Sheila* delivers it to the customers.

Nothing is missing here. In **World Beta**, Fred doesn’t exist, and so Bob has figured out how to do all that work himself. **Here’s what that looks like:**

**So in World Beta, here’s how it works:**

1. Bob sells the brew-ready barley & hops to Stan for **$1.50.**
2. Stan turns what he got from Bob into Beer, and he sells it to Shiela for **$3.00.**
3. Sheila then delivers the beer, and since her costs are so much lower, she sells it to the happy customer for the price of **$4.50.**

Voila! **We have saved a whopping $2.00 per beer!**

Perhaps you’ve heard the phrase:

> *“****Let’s Cut Out The Middle Man”***

Well, this is done to eliminate transaction costs. Simply put, by eliminating the number of times a process changes hands of ownership, the overall cost of the process is reduced. This is why most beer companies try to do as many of the above steps of the beermaking process as they can — it’s just not always feasible or practical to do so.

## So That’s What Causes Inflation?

Nope. Sorry, it’s really not that simple. Transactions costs are, however, what will guarantee that inflation will occur. As long as there are agents along the supply chain who need to profit, prices will go up, and this will reduce the value of currency over time.

### But what else causes inflation?

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*MDHH-XqmaT7Evs7g.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

Well… if a government fails to properly manage the money supply, then the rate at which the money supply in the economy increases can outpace the actual growth of the economy. If this happens, then we will experience what is called **sustained inflation.**

> ***Sustained inflation is when prices increase because the money that is used to buy things becomes less valuable, and this causes prices along the process of production to go up.***

Think of it this way. If you have $1,000, but the value of it drops, in order to sustain your existing quality of life, you need to earn more to make up for that. Right? If you own a business, you’re going to increase the prices of your goods/services in order to make up for the loss from your currency going down in value.

Now, if you apply this concept to our worlds of beer mentioned above, each one of those people in that process needs to increase the price of what they sell to the next person.

In other words, Farmer Bob can’t pay his bills for $1.50 anymore and needs to ask the next person in the next node to pay a little more.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*iFL5uv6JuMlKi5nH.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

## Putting It All Together

So you may be wondering, “I thought this article was about the **Time Value of Money?** Yes, it is. The reason understanding inflation is important is because that’s the primary motivator for believing in the time value of money.

What do I mean by “believing?”

The Time Value of Money is a theory. Money itself does not exist in nature so there are no laws that say that money must lose value over time. However, money is created by people, and people do, in fact, lose value over time.

**“Did he just say that?” Yes, yes I did. Allow me to explain:**

People die, and so the most important moments we have are, well, right now! The future is uncertain, and because the future might never come, or might not turn out the way we expect, the least risky time to spend money is right now. See? I bet you never knew Finance was so deep.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*okvbyg7d_6xPoGWS.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

The Time Value of Money is based on the concept that sacrificing now for the future comes with inherent risk. However, there is a good chance that we will survive to that point in the future when things could be better!

In other words, it’s a risk to bet on the future, but it’s also a risk not to!

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*uCtHykIrMTH7-7px.gif)

Courtesy [GIPHY](https://giphy.com/)

In order to minimize the risk of making the wrong decisions, either way, investors put a lot of time and energy into analyzing what will happen in the future. How do they do this? Well… this article is about WHY the Time Value of Money is a thing — we get into HOW it works in other articles. For now, just remember this:

> ***The entire finance and business world believes that money today is worth more than money tomorrow, so get with the program and use that mattress for something smarter.***

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/0*wM4wuAlUqK8f_sR2.gif)

Of course, you do, and that’s okay. Finance Fools will answer all of them in time. For now, here’s a preemptive checklist to calm your anxieties:

- Yes, deflation can occur, and it can be good, and it can also be ruinous because of the effect it has on the value of existing assets.
- Yes, the concepts of time value of money are old, and yes, they will likely be around for a long time.
- No, you cannot change the world and change everyone’s mind.
- Yes, it’s much much more complex than this, which is why we cover the topic across many different articles.
- Yes, if you talk about this with your friends they’ll be like:

At a state school in Oregon, a professor of philosophy decided to write 20 bogus-satire-fueled papers that were aimed at exposing just how ridiculous the academic industrial complex truly is — the results were both horrifying and hilarious.

Three scholars — James Lindsay, Helen Pluckrose, and Peter Boghossian — worked together to write the 20 fake papers using trendy academic jargon to make the argument for absolutely ridiculous conclusions. They then submitted the articles to high-profile journals in fields including gender studies, queer studies, and fat studies — the success rate was remarkable.

Of the 20 outlandish papers, seven of the articles had been outright accepted for publication in peer-reviewed journals, and seven more had been accepted for continuing through the various stages of the publishing review process.

Only *six* of the fake papers actually were rejected.

How ridiculous and absurd were these papers? Well:

- Western Astronomy is Sexist and Imperialist — Physics Departments Should Study Feminist Astrology, or Practice Interpretative Dance.
- Human Reaction to Rape Culture and Queer Performativity at Urban Dog Parks in Portland, Oregon.
- Rubbing One Out: Defining Metasexual Violence of Objectification Through Nonconsensual Masturbation

If the titles are not stupid enough, here is an actual excerpt from one of the published papers:

> “By drawing upon empirical studies of psychological harms of objectification, especially through depersonalization, and exploring several veins of theoretical literature on nonphysical forms of sexual violence, this articles seeks to situate non-consensual male autoerotic fantasizing about women as a form of metatextual violence that depersonalizes her, injures her being on an affective level, contributes to consequent harms of objectification and rape culture, and can appropriate her identity for the purpose of male sexual gratification.”

The paper argues that argues that men who masturbate while thinking about a woman without her prior consent are perpetrators of sexual violence. This article was published — this is the result of leftist woke philosophy in academia — those who speak out against it be damned.

Professor Boghossian, the mastermind behind writing the fake papers and thus exposing leftist academia for what it is, resigned from his university on September 8th.

In his resignation letter, Boghossian wrote that his prior employer had been changed from,

> “a bastion of free inquiry into a Social Justice factory whose only inputs were race, gender and victimhood and whose only outputs were grievance and division”.

And that students at the university are,

> “being trained to mimic the moral certainty of ideologues.”

Many other scholars around the western world have found themselves in similar situations. What few conservatives who remain in academia may be used to this type of nonsense, the illiberalism of academia is now increasingly affecting actual liberals — people who believe in progress but don’t believe the woke tooth fairy.

[A report published recently found that since 2015](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2021/09/25/a-professors-resignation-highlights-pressures-within-academia-to-conform) the number of scholars targeted with demands for investigation, demotion, censorship, or termination reached an all time high in 2020. The majority of these cases are academics on the left being attacked from further left.

The censoring had bled into medicine and actual hard sciences — last year a cardiologist at the University of Pittsburgh was demoted for publishing a paper in the *Journal of the American Heart Association* that analyzed and criticized diversity initiatives, and another professor at a University of California med school recently apologized for using the term *pregnant women*.

One of Boghossian’s supporters in Oregon stated that the *diversity industrial complex* within college administrations always needs to to justify their existence — the result is outlandish social justice policing that creates more and more social laws to enforce.

It’s a lucrative gig: Diversity Vice-Presidents at universities in Oregon have an average compensation of $262,000. The University of Michigan has nearly 80 diversity officers at a total annual payroll cost of $10.6m. Anyone who wants to challenge prevailing views faces an onslaught of self-perseveration from those whose job it is to persecute those who are racist and sexist.

Social justice is now big business, and just the very notion of being pursued by the diversity and inclusion thought police is enough to silence people.
