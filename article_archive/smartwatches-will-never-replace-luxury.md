### Financial Crime | Cryptocurrency Crash

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*Rx9bhtuXwzaK1zZGwEcJwg.png)

Photo by [Kristy Pargeter](http://www.kjpargeterimages.co.uk/)

New Jersey-based [Celsius Network LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius_Network), a significant player in cryptocurrency lending, filed for bankruptcy on July 13, 2022, in the Southern District of New York, a mere one month after blocking all customer withdrawals. This occurred one week after the bankruptcy filing of cryptocurrency competitor, [Voyager Digital Holdings Inc.](https://www.investvoyager.com/investorrelations/overview/)

Interestingly enough, both companies filed in the exact same court, and both have locked up literally billions of dollars worth of assets belonging to their customers.

Celsius was founded in 2017 by famed and self-righteous tech entrepreneur [Alex Mashinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Mashinsky), serial angel investor [S. Daniel Leon](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sdanielleon/), and some guy who goes by the name of [“Nuke” Goldstein.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nukegold/)

Celsius and Voyager Digital are both [cryptocurrency exchange](https://www.investopedia.com/best-crypto-exchanges-5071855) companies that loan people real money (aka fiat currency) in exchange for them parking their precious digital currencies onto company-controlled digital servers.

Celsius, one of the world’s largest global cryptocurrency platforms, has positioned itself as a digital platform where customers can “buy, swap, earn, borrow and send crypto — all in one app, with zero fees.” In short, Celsius was a darling of the blockchain world and promised to finally deliver the revolutionary experience that so many crypto enthusiasts have been eagerly anticipating.

Unfortunately for the customers of Celsius, [the company suddenly froze all customer withdrawals and lending activities, including for corporate customers](https://www.wsj.com/articles/celsius-defends-decision-to-halt-withdrawals-at-debut-bankruptcy-hearing-11658189510), and subsequently went belly up.

Why?

### It’s getting hot in here

Celsius was officially launched in 2018 and managed to quickly open $50 million worth of customer accounts. By May of 2019, customers had transferred over $200 million into their Celsius accounts; less than two years later, that number had grown to more than $10 billion.

As a result of this insane growth, in May of 2022, Celsius raised an additional $690 million, at a company valuation of $3 billion.

If this seems fast to you, it’s because it’s almost unheard of and defies the very laws of financial physics.

So why did Celsius grow so fast?

The primary reason is that Celsius claimed to have solved a major problem for cryptocurrency enthusiasts — liquidity.

Despite many creative attempts at making it more liquid, cryptocurrency has remained very difficult to exchange with other assets and fiat currencies. The founders of Celsius believed that crypto asset holders needed a way to convert their cryptocurrency assets into useable currency, aka real money, and that the best way to do this was to set up a platform where crypto users and investors could find each other and make exchanges.

Essentially, Celsius wanted to be an online market exchange.

While that model seemed neat enough, the founders of Celsius wanted more, and so they figured that they could entice crypto users who would jump at the chance to generate yields off their existing holdings, similar to how a savings account at a bank works.

Simply put, people with Celsius accounts could transfer their crypto assets to other Celsius users in exchange for fiat currency, **or** (and this is a big or), they could lend their crypto assets directly to Celsius itself for yearly interest.

Got that?

To sweeten the deal, customers could opt to have Celsius pay the interest in other cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Etherium. When customers chose this interest-generating option, they would receive [CEL tokens](https://celsius.network/cel-token-explained), which if you haven’t already figured out, stands for “Celsius.”

I know what you’re asking yourself, “so could users exchange the CEL Tokens on other exchanges?” And yes, if that’s what you were wondering, they could! There is, indeed, a secondary market for CEL tokens.

Now, if you’re thinking to yourself, “but that’s crazy because then the value of CEL could be affected by outside market influences so my interest won’t be guaranteed,” then you obviously paid attention in kindergarten.

Unfortunately, the cryptocurrency world is loaded with people who subscribe to the rocket-ship-moon-emoji culture, and so they tend to jump at any creative ideas that promise to squeeze a bit more value out of something that doesn’t actually exist in the first place.

It gets worse.

CEL Tokens entitled their holders to certain perks on the platform and effectively functioned as debtor notes that also offered a weird type of loyalty and rewards program, which in turn provided certain customers with highly favourable rates.

Which customers? We’ll get to that in a moment.

Simply put, CEL Tokens were issued in order to reduce the liability that Celsius had to its customers, and so they incentivised customers to take CEL Tokens by making the tokens a tradable asset on digital currency exchanges, and further by offering loyalty rewards in the form of better interest rates, which in turn would likely be used to issue more CEL Tokens.

If that doesn’t make sense to you, don't worry, it’s because you aren’t crazy.

If it *does* make sense, then you probably also know that incentivizing customers to take on a bogus asset in place of actual money in an effort to avoid having to pay your liabilities all at once is, what we call, a [Ponzi Scheme](https://www.investor.gov/protect-your-investments/fraud/types-fraud/ponzi-scheme#:~:text=A%20Ponzi%20scheme%20is%20an,do%20not%20invest%20the%20money.).

### Promises Promises

Of course, the lawsuits are piling up and social media is illuminated with shocked and outraged crypto users, all of whom have been warned time and time again of the dangers of navigating within unregulated markets.

Regardless of what is good and right and decent, in the end, Celsius might actually win all of the lawsuits against it, because it engineered an audacious and blatantly criminal Terms of Use agreement that clearly states that customers aren’t actually customers, they are investors.

Yup.

Celsius very clearly stated in their filing in New York that upon utilizing the rewards programs, users transferred “all right and title” to their crypto assets to Celsius and basically gave Celsius carte blanche to do with those assets what they pleased.

It gets worse when you read [the language from Celsius’ Terms of Use](https://substack.com/redirect/bb428191-0f51-4a67-8f5c-6245ac9435b1?u=6384750):

> In consideration for the Rewards payable to you on the Eligible Digital Assets using the Earn Service, for us entering into any Loan Agreement, and the use of our Services, you grant Celsius, subject to applicable law and for the duration of the period during which you elect to utilize the Eligible Digital Assets in the Earn Service (if available to you) and thus loan such Eligible Digital Assets to us through your Celsius Account, or as collateral under the Borrow Service (if available to you), all right and title to such Eligible Digital Assets, including ownership rights, and the right, without further notice to you, to hold such Digital Assets in Celsius’ own Virtual Wallet or elsewhere, and to pledge, re-pledge, hypothecate, rehypothecate, sell, lend, or otherwise transfer or use any amount of such Digital Assets, separately or together with other property, with all attendant rights of ownership, and for any period of time, and without retaining in Celsius’ possession and/or control a like amount of Digital Assets or any other monies or assets, and to use or invest such Digital Assets in Celsius’ full discretion. You acknowledge that with respect to Digital Assets used by Celsius pursuant to this paragraph:

> You will not be able to exercise rights of ownership;

> Celsius may receive compensation in connection with lending or otherwise using Digital Assets in its business to which you have no claim or entitlement; and

> In the event that Celsius becomes bankrupt, enters liquidation or is otherwise unable to repay its obligations, any Eligible Digital Assets used in the Earn Service or as collateral under the Borrow Service may not be recoverable, and you may not have any legal remedies or rights in connection with Celsius’ obligations to you other than your rights as a creditor of Celsius under any applicable laws.

Now that’s some fine print for ages.

In short, this wordy agreement says that customers don’t own any of the assets that exist within their Celsius accounts — Celsius actually got their customers to give up ownership of anything they put into their accounts.

If you are getting angry right now, that means you understood.

Imagine a bank telling you that once you deposit your money into a checking account, it’s no longer yours. Better yet, imagine next the bank tells you that they will reward you the longer you keep that money in your account because they are going to use it as collateral to take other loans which they will use to juice sick yields off of other investments that you won’t ever benefit from.

This is essentially what Celsius did, but it’s worse because, unlike a typical bank, Celsius actually incentivised users to put all of their different types of digital assets into Celsius accounts.

Remember the rewards and loyalty programs?

When Celsius saw an opportunity to profit by exchanging one currency for another, they would quickly jack up the rewards for that currency on the customer accounts so that people would deposit more of that currency and retain existing assets of said currency into their Celsius accounts.

Unbeknownst to the customer, since the asset was now legally the property of Celsius (per the Terms Of Use agreement), they would flip that currency to realize a fast profit.

For example, imagine that Celsius saw that they could swap [Dogecoin](https://dogecoin.com/) for USD (U.S. Dollar) at a favourable rate, and so they offered incentives to customers to deposit all their Dogecoin into Celsius accounts. Next, Celsius makes the trade and profits big time — simple. The problem with this is that now Celsius customers can’t easily withdraw Dogecoin, because Celsius no longer has any, since they traded it for USD.

In traditional banks, this practice is highly illegal, but in the cryptocurrency world, anything goes.

It gets worse — each time Celsius did this, the customer rewards would remain in place until Celsius could replenish their supply of the cryptocurrency in the event of a demand for withdrawals, thus inflating the value of the currency, even though supply didn’t exist.

Unfortunately for customers, this business model depended upon the entire crypto world not disintegrating overnight, which we all know actually happened very recently.

Voyager Digital followed a similar business model as Celsius and thus was brought to its knees in a similar way. What Voyager Digital did not have, however, was the concept that they outright owned any assets that customers dared deposit into their accounts, and this is probably why Voyager became insolvent before Celsius.

Honesty and ethics can certainly be a bitch.

### Blame it on the other guy

Both Voyager Digital and Celsius stole money from unsuspecting customers after lying to them about the integrity of their respective companies, and both firms promised their users that they would be able to access their assets when needed, similar to a real bank. Despite that, both companies quickly froze all customer accounts when the companies themselves faced insolvency, and both blamed each other for their woes.

In other words, rather than lose their business, they opted to fuck their customers out of all of the assets that they promised to protect, and rather than own up to their mistakes, they opted to blame the world and their competitors.

The reality is that both of these companies operated like criminal syndicates, and that is why they failed.

Celsius essentially turned its customers into unwilling and unsecured creditors, while Voyager Digital just simply lied about users being able to withdraw money at any time, “no questions asked.”

Regardless, both CEOs appear to have no shame.

From Twitter — Voyager Digital CEO blatantly lying about the policy his company has regarding withdrawals.

Additionally, Celsius released a statement after filing Chapter 11:

> The onset of the “crypto winter” combined with the well-publicized collapse of Luna and the failure of several crypto funds/exchanges led to growing industry-wide reluctance to do business with companies, such as Celsius, that held crypto assets. This reluctance was exacerbated by a series of negative media and social media comments about Celsius, a number of which were unsupported and misleading. As a result of all of these factors, users began withdrawing crypto from Celsius’ platform in large amounts and at a rapid pace.

All of this is untrue and dismisses the real reason why the business failed — Celsius was extremely over-leveraged in basically every possible way.

For example, only 27% of Celsius’ ETH1 (Ethereum) holding was liquid, meaning that, on any given day, they could have at best paid only 1 out of 4 of their customers the full balance of Ethereum currency in their existing accounts. Ethereum was, indeed, one of the largest cryptocurrency holdings on the platform.

In other words, Celsius opened a bunch of loans, and then took user deposits and traded them for cryptocurrency, which resulted in them owing a lot of money and not having the reserves to pay those loans back.

Simply put, Celsius is insolvent, and always has been — but don’t believe me, just look at their Balance Sheet as of one week ago:

### Broke again, but not as broke as the customer

Today, Celsius continues to freeze all accounts and activity, and its customers will likely lose everything.

In any event, as you can see, customers entrusted the Celsius platform, as well as Voyager Digital, with a wealth of assets. With wealth comes power and responsibility, and it appears that, in no uncertain terms, both Voyager Digital and Celsius brazenly shirked that responsibility.

The cryptocurrency crash has shown the world that governments must step in and regulate digital coins in a major way, and soon, as this latest event with major crypto exchanges is just the next chapter in the ongoing saga of unbelievable financial crime taking place online.

While most ordinary people tend to steer clear of get-rich-quick ventures, when those avoidable schemes threaten to blow up the unavoidable and legitimate economy that we all depend upon for day-to-day survival, well, then it’s time to do something. That time is now.
